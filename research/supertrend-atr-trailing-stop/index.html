Supertrend vs ATR Trailing Stop vs Chandelier Exit: Backtested

Supertrend (SUPER-01), the ATR trailing stop (RISK-04) and Chandelier Exit (TREND-17) ended with almost the same average full-period Sharpe ratio (0.38, 0.41 and 0.40) before trading costs, but they entered the market 1,973, 4,852 and 8,320 times. Much of the difference is how quickly each one re-enters after an exit.

SupertrendATRTrailing StopChandelier Exit

Three stops, one idea

All three follow a price with a stop set a few average daily ranges below it, and leave the market when the close falls through. All three are long only here: when stopped out they hold cash, not a short. Results are before trading costs, for the last five years to 2026-11-11 and the full period (2010-01-04 for most markets, 2014-09-17 for bitcoin, 2017-11-09 for ether).

The differences are in the details. They decide how often each one trades.

What each rule actually does

Supertrend (SUPER-01) reads real daily highs, lows and closes. It builds a band around the day's midpoint, 2.5 times a smoothed average true range (exponential average, span 10). The lower band can only rise. The system leaves when the close drops below it and comes back only when the close breaks above the upper band.

ATR trailing stop (RISK-04) enters whenever the close is above its 20-day average. The stop is the highest close since entry minus 2.5 times "ATR", and it only moves up. The code's ATR is a close-to-close version: the 14-day simple average of the absolute change from one close to the next, with no highs, lows or gaps. A day's true range is never smaller than that move, so this version measures a smaller range and the stop tends to sit tighter. After an exit it can re-enter the next day if the close is back above the average.

Chandelier Exit (TREND-17) uses the same close-to-close ATR over 22 days. The stop is the 22-day highest close minus 3 times that ATR. It re-enters when the close rises above the 22-day lowest close plus 3 ATR, a low bar, which fits its 8,320 entries.

Best, worst and the three-way tally

Supertrend's best market was gold over five years and ether over the full period (Sharpe 1.00); its worst were EUR/USD over five years and natural gas over the full period (−15.3% a year, −94.2% drawdown). The ATR stop's best were gold over five years and bitcoin over the full period (Sharpe 1.09); its worst were natural gas and EUR/USD respectively. Supertrend had a positive annual return on eight markets over five years and seven over the full period; the ATR stop on nine and eight.

Head to head, the ATR stop had the higher five-year Sharpe on eight of ten markets (mean 0.38 vs 0.25), but over the full period the gap shrank to 0.41 vs 0.38, with Supertrend ahead on four. Supertrend's edge was drawdown: shallower on nine of ten markets over the full period (mean −43.2% vs −48.1%). Chandelier had a five-year mean Sharpe of 0.42 and a full-period mean of 0.40.

Why the trade counts differ so much

Across the ten markets, entries since 2010 were 1,973 for Supertrend, 4,852 for the ATR stop and 8,320 for Chandelier. Dividing days in the market by entries gives roughly 10.9 days per holding for Supertrend, 4.8 for the ATR stop and 2.7 for Chandelier on average. On gold, Chandelier made 954 entries.

The reason is the re-entry rule. Supertrend needs a close above a band that sits 2.5 ATR above the midpoint. The other two re-enter on a lower bar, so a stop-out is often followed by another entry within days. On a given day Supertrend and the ATR stop agree on being in or out a median 81% of the time; the ATR stop and Chandelier agree 87%.

The worst calendar year in each market

In each market's worst full calendar year, all three beat the market in every case; the average market fell 42.8% in those years against average results of −10.0% for Supertrend, −15.9% for the ATR stop and −3.0% for Chandelier. On US stocks in 2022, when the market fell 18.2%, they lost 5.1%, 4.6% and 2.8%.

Oil in 2020 (−67.8%) shows the spread: the ATR stop gained 2.4%, Chandelier 12.4% and Supertrend lost 28.2%. Natural gas in 2023 (−64.0%) hurt all three: −42.7%, −52.3% and −17.4%. Ether in 2018 (−82.4%) is the odd one: Supertrend gained 47.8% and Chandelier 40.8%, while the ATR stop lost 38.2%. That year falls in each market's first three years, which use default parameters, and Supertrend's gain includes 30.3% in January alone.

What the numbers cannot tell you

Costs are the first caveat. The ATR stop and Chandelier trade far more often than Supertrend (4,852 and 8,320 entries against 1,973), and none of that friction is in these results. Average holdings of 4.8 and 2.7 days also mean these two behave less like trailing a trend and more like a fast filter.

The second is that three very similar-looking results on average can hide large differences by market, and bitcoin and ether have short histories, starting 2014-09-17 and 2017-11-09. None of this is a promise of what the next stretch will show.

Takeaways

  • Average Sharpe was nearly identical across the three stops; the differences are in trading frequency and drawdown.
  • Supertrend traded least and had a shallower full-period drawdown than the ATR stop on nine of ten markets.
  • The ATR stop's "ATR" is a close-to-close version, which makes its stop tighter and its trading faster.
  • All three cut the loss in each market's worst calendar year, but still lost money in most of them.
