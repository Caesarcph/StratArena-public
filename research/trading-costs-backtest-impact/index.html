The test

We took all 576 strategy-and-market results over the five years to 3 November 2026: 72 strategies on up to 10 markets, all with walk-forward optimization. For each one we estimate the cost of trading from its turnover: every time the position changes by 100% of the account, we charge 10 basis points (0.10%). That covers commission, the bid-ask spread and some slippage, a middle-of-the-road figure for a retail trader. Large ETFs and major currency pairs can be cheaper; crypto and thinner markets usually cost more.

The cost is subtracted from the annual return. This is an approximation, but a close one at these sizes, and the same one the cost slider on every strategy page uses.

The typical cost: 1.2 points a year

The median result changed position 82 times in five years and paid about 1.2 percentage points a year at 10 basis points. One in ten paid 2.9 points or more, and the busiest paid 6.8.

That sounds small, but most strategy returns are small too. Among the 340 results that made more than 1% a year, the cost took a median of 20% of the return. For 60 of them (18%) it took half or more.

Profitable results that stop being profitable

380 of the 576 results had a positive annual return before costs. Charging for trading turned some of them into losers:

  • At 5 basis points: 34 became flat or negative
  • At 10 basis points: 53
  • At 20 basis points: 94
  • At 30 basis points: 149, nearly two in five

The same happens to the results that look best. Before costs, 195 results beat simply holding the market over five years. After 10 basis points 163 still did, after 20 basis points 145, and after 30 basis points 129. On the S&P 500 the bar was already high: 2 of 62 strategies beat holding the index before costs, and only one after.

Who pays the most

Cost depends almost entirely on how often a strategy trades. A few examples on the S&P 500 over five years:

  • TREND-17 Chandelier Exit made 6.1% a year before costs from 119 position changes, about 3.8% after
  • MR-16 Connors RSI made 7.9% a year from 152 changes, about 4.8% after
  • HMA-01 HMA Crossover was already flat before costs (−0.1%) and lost about 3.9% a year after
  • TREND-10 Ichimoku Cloud traded 59 times and kept 5.3% of its 6.5%

By family, volatility strategies traded the most (a median of 151 position changes in five years) and paid 1.7 points a year. Regime strategies paid 2.2. Trend and mean-reversion strategies paid about 1.2 each, but that hurts mean reversion far more: its median result made only 0.8% a year, so the cost was larger than the return. The cheapest strategies to run were slow allocation rules such as dual momentum and asset-class rotation, which change position only a few times a year.

What to take from it

  • Read every backtest as "before costs" unless it says otherwise, and check how often the strategy trades.
  • At 10 basis points, a strategy that changes position about 30 times a year needs roughly 3 points of extra return just to break even with a version that never trades.
  • Fast strategies on small edges are the first to disappear. Slow strategies with larger edges survive costs best.
  • Use your own numbers. Every strategy page has a cost slider from 0 to 50 basis points.

These are backtests, not investment advice.
