Does Buying the Dip Work? Four Dip Rules on Ten Markets

After a fall of 3% or more in five days, inside an uptrend, the average 21-day return was +1.0% against +0.7% on all days (medians over nine markets, 2010-2026): higher in 8 of 9 markets but statistically clear in only one, and the worst case, US stocks on 2020-02-24, lost 24.1% over the next 21 days.

One saying, four ways to test it

"Buy the dip" has no single definition, so the claim checker tests several. The main one is a fall of 3% or more over five trading days while the close is still above its 200-day average. Three related sayings are tested alongside it: three down closes in a row, RSI(14) below 30, and a 20-day fall of 10% or more below the 200-day average (the "falling knife"). A fifth idea, buying when the drawdown from the peak reaches -20%, was also run.

Each is an event study on ten markets: US stocks, gold, silver, oil, natural gas, broad commodities, agriculture, EUR/USD, bitcoin and ether. Daily closes run from 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-11, with bitcoin from 2014-09-17 and ether from 2017-11-09. Everything is measured before trading costs.

The main test

When the signal appears at a close, the test records the return over the next 21 trading days. Events must be at least 21 trading days apart, so that one slump is not counted many times, and the result is compared with the average 21-day return of the same market on all days. That comparison matters: it removes the fact that most of these markets rose over the period.

Across the nine markets with enough events (436 events in total), the median 21-day return after the signal was +1.0% against +0.7% on all days. It was above the market's own average in 8 of 9 markets, and statistically clear, with at least 20 events and the same sign in both halves of the history, in 1.

  • US stocks (38 events): +2.1% against +1.2%; 71% of events positive against 69% of all windows.
  • Broad commodities (39 events): +2.1% against +0.4%, 74% positive against 54%. This is the one clear result.
  • Bitcoin (82 events) and ether (56 events): +5.1% against +4.0% and +4.4% against +3.6%.
  • Silver (56 events) and oil (60 events): no different from usual, +0.9% against +0.9% and +0.3% against +0.3%.
  • Agriculture (24 events): +0.1% against +0.2%, the only market below its average.

EUR/USD had only 4 events and could not be judged.

The same fall below the 200-day average

The same 3% five-day fall, but with the close below the 200-day average, looks very different. Across 10 markets and 609 events the median was +0.4% against +0.5% on all days; it was above the market's average in 4 markets and below in 6, with none clear and stable. For crypto the gap was large: bitcoin +1.7% against +4.0% (68 events) and ether +0.5% against +3.6% (62 events).

EUR/USD was the exception, +1.3% against -0.1% with a t-statistic of 2.72 over 17 events, but it did not hold in both halves of the history, so the checker does not count it. The trend filter is doing some work, though a sample of this size cannot say how much.

Three other dip rules

Three down closes in a row, measured over the next 3 days (2,580 events, ten markets): the median was 0.0% against +0.1% on all days, above usual in only 3 markets. In gold (234 events) and silver (230 events) the result was clearly worse than usual, at -0.1% and -0.4% against +0.1% for both.

RSI(14) below 30, measured over 10 days (441 events): the median was +0.1% against +0.2%, split 5 markets above and 5 below. US stocks looked good at +1.9% against +0.6% (22 events), bitcoin too at +3.4% against +1.8% (48 events), but oil was -2.1% against +0.1% (55 events) and ether -2.1% against +1.6% (32 events). Nothing was clear.

The falling knife (a 20-day fall of 10% or more below the 200-day average, 21-day return) is the opposite saying. Across the six markets with enough events (253 events) the median was -0.7% against +0.7%, lower than usual in 5 of 6, but none clear. Ether was -1.0% against +3.6% (46 events). Buying when the drawdown reached -20% produced at most 9 events in any market, too few to judge.

The worst stretches for dip buyers

Averages hide the bad cases. The largest loss after a signal in US stocks came on 2020-02-24, the first dip signal of the pandemic fall, which was followed by -24.1% over 21 trading days, while the market fell 33.7% from 2020-02-19 to 2020-03-23. The next three signals in 2020 returned +5.2%, +2.7% and +11.3%.

Bitcoin's worst event was 2020-02-26, at -40.6%. Averaged over each market's five worst events, the next 21 days lost 26.9% in bitcoin, 27.4% in ether, 23.4% in natural gas (worst: -31.9% on 2022-05-31) and 8.8% in US stocks. The share of events followed by a loss was 29% in US stocks and 26% in broad commodities, but 53% in oil, 54% in agriculture and 57% in natural gas.

A dip-buying strategy on the site

The nearest site strategy is RSI Mean Reversion (MR-01). Its code holds the market only on days when the 14-day RSI, calculated with simple averages, is below 30; it does not wait for RSI to recover. Over the full stored history, which for US stocks starts in 2010, it was in the market between 4.6% of days (US stocks) and 16.1% (natural gas). Its Sharpe ratio was above buy-and-hold in only 2 of 10 markets, natural gas and agriculture.

In US stocks it earned 3.5% a year against 13.9% for buy-and-hold, with a maximum drawdown of -12.3% against -33.7%. Mostly sitting in cash lowers risk, but it also gives up the return of a market that mostly rose.

What the numbers cannot tell you

The main test and the falling-knife test are rated "partly" by the checker, the three-down-days and RSI tests "does not hold", and the -20% drawdown test is too small to judge. One clear result among nine markets is within what chance can produce when this many tests are run. The period 2010-2026 includes long rises in US stocks, crypto and gold, which flatters any rule that buys.

Events from one slump are not independent. The 2020 and 2022 sell-offs account for several of the worst outcomes. All figures are before costs, and the "dip" is defined at the close, so no intraday entry is assumed.

Takeaways

  • A 3% five-day fall in an uptrend was followed by a slightly better-than-usual month in 8 of 9 markets (+1.0% against +0.7% median), but only broad commodities were clear.
  • The same fall below the 200-day average, three down closes in a row and RSI below 30 did not beat the usual return.
  • The worst outcomes were large (-24.1% in US stocks, -40.6% in bitcoin), so the average is a poor guide to a single trade.
