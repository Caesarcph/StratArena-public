Does Buying When RSI Is Below 30 Work? A Ten-Market Backtest

RSI Mean Reversion (MR-01) is long only on days when RSI is below 30; the code never holds a position until RSI recovers. That left it invested on 5.9% of days on US stocks over five years, with a 5.2% annual return and a 7.2% maximum drawdown, against 11.9% and 24.5% for holding.

The rule as coded

RSI Mean Reversion (MR-01) computes a 14-day RSI from simple averages of the daily gains and losses. On any day when that RSI is below 30, the position for the next day is long, at 100%. On every other day the position is zero.

The strategy page describes a different rule: buy when RSI is below 30 and volatility is low, then exit when RSI rises above 50. The code has no memory and no volatility filter. It does contain a line that sets the position to zero when RSI is above 50, but a day with RSI above 50 is already flat, so that line never changes anything. The strategy is therefore long only while RSI is still below 30, and it steps out as soon as RSI ticks back to 30 or higher. It has no parameters, so the walk-forward step has nothing to choose. All results here are before trading costs, and a position decided at the close of day t earns the return of day t+1.

How few days it is invested

On US stocks RSI was below 30 on only 4.6% of days over the full history. That produced 78 entries, averaging 2.5 days each, and 41% of them lasted a single day. On the other nine markets RSI was below 30 on between 10.1% (bitcoin) and 16.3% (natural gas) of days. Pooled across the ten markets, the average holding period was 4.0 days, 37% of holds lasted one day and the longest was 38 days. Over the last five years the average share of days invested was 9.7%.

For comparison, we also ran the rule the description implies: buy below 30 and hold until RSI is above 50. This is not a strategy the site tests, and it is our own calculation. On US stocks it would have held for 12.2 days on average and been invested on 18% of days over five years, returning 4.8% a year with a Sharpe ratio of 0.48 and a -12.9% maximum drawdown. The coded rule did better on all three: 5.2%, 0.77 and -7.2%.

Results across ten markets

Over the five years to 11 November 2026, and before costs, the coded rule had a positive Sharpe ratio and a positive annual return on all 10 markets, beat the Sharpe ratio of holding on 7 (US stocks, gold, oil, natural gas, EUR/USD, bitcoin and ether), and had a smaller maximum drawdown than holding on all 10.

  • Oil: 9.1% a year, Sharpe 0.99, maximum drawdown -8.4%, against 20.3%, 0.68 and -36.2% for holding.
  • Gold: 4.8% a year, Sharpe 0.91, drawdown -6.0%, against 16.0%, 0.87 and -28.3%.
  • Ether: 12.2% a year, Sharpe 0.46, drawdown -38.0%, while holding lost 15.4% a year.
  • Broad commodities and agriculture: the weakest, at 0.1% and 0.6% a year.

Much of the five-year appeal is the small drawdown, and the longer record is much weaker. Over the full period (from January 2010 for US stocks, September 2014 for bitcoin and November 2017 for ether) the Sharpe ratio was positive on only 6 of 10 markets and above holding's on 2. US stocks returned 3.5% a year with a Sharpe of 0.51 and a -12.3% drawdown, against 13.9%, 0.85 and -33.7%. Bitcoin returned 5.9% a year against 52.0% for holding.

A bad stretch: oil, 2014 to 2020

The worst drawdown was on oil. From the peak on 12 May 2014 to the trough on 28 April 2020 the strategy fell 77.5%; holding the fund over the same dates fell 94.2%. It was invested on 291 of those 1,502 trading days.

In sharp crashes, buying oversold readings early is costly. In the 2020 sell-off (19 February to 23 March) the strategy lost 4.8% on US stocks, against 33.7% for holding, and 25.3% on bitcoin, against 33.4%. In 2022 (3 January to 12 October) it gained 8.9% on US stocks while holding lost 24.5%, and gained 11.7% on bitcoin while holding lost 58.8%. A rule that is invested one day in ten can look very different depending on whether the few days it chooses fall in a crash.

Connors RSI and Bollinger Band Reversion, which do hold

Connors RSI (MR-16) averages a 3-day RSI, an RSI of the up/down streak length and a percentile rank of the daily return over 100 days. It buys when that composite is below 10 and exits when it is above 50 or after 10 days. Bollinger Band Reversion (MR-02) buys when the close is at or below the lower band and the bands are wider than their 20-day average, and exits at the middle band, after a maximum holding period, or on a stop 3% below the lower band.

Their holding behaviour differs. Pooled over the ten markets, Connors RSI held 2.3 days on average (the maximum is 10) and Bollinger Band Reversion 8.0 days, with only 6% of its holds lasting one day. Over five years the average number of trades per market (each entry and each exit counts as one) was 80 for MR-01, 156 for Connors RSI and 38 for Bollinger Band Reversion; the share of days invested was 9.7%, 12.8% and 10.6%.

On US stocks over five years Connors RSI was the strongest of the three: 7.9% a year, Sharpe 1.03, drawdown -7.6%, against 5.2%, 0.77 and -7.2% for MR-01 and 3.3%, 0.42 and -9.0% for Bollinger Band Reversion. Across all ten markets it was weaker: a positive Sharpe on 6, against 10 for MR-01 and 8 for Bollinger Band Reversion. At the site's cost assumption of 10 basis points per unit of turnover, Connors RSI costs 3.0% a year on US stocks, against 1.2% for MR-01 and 0.7% for Bollinger Band Reversion.

What the numbers cannot tell you

Small samples dominate. MR-01 made 60 trades on US stocks in five years, counting each entry and each exit, which is about 30 round trips. The probabilistic Sharpe ratio (the probability that the true Sharpe is above zero) was 0.99 for US stocks, but the deflated Sharpe ratio, which adjusts for the number of strategies compared, was 0.63. The five-year window was also kinder than the full history, in which the result on oil was a loss of 3.2% a year.

The site's ranking also treats such a strategy awkwardly. The Arena Score only counts a strategy with a non-zero return on at least 5% of days, and on the full-period US stocks cohort MR-01 was invested on 4.6%, so its score there is 0.0. On the five-year window, with 5.9% invested, the same strategy scores 77.34. A rule that is rarely invested can post a small drawdown simply by being absent.

Takeaways

  • MR-01 is a rebound bet on days when RSI is below 30. The code never holds a position until RSI recovers to 50, whatever the strategy page says.
  • It was invested on about 10% of days over five years, so its small drawdowns say more about time in cash than about timing.
  • The five-year record is better than the full history: positive Sharpe on 10 markets in five years but 6 in the full period.
  • The two relatives that hold positions behave differently: Connors RSI trades about twice as often and had the best US stock result of the three, but a positive Sharpe on only 6 markets; Bollinger Band Reversion trades least.
