We Backtested 56 Trading Strategies on Gold. Only 3 Beat Simply Holding It.

Gold more than doubled between November 2021 and November 2026, then fell 28% from its January 2026 peak. Over those five years only three of 56 rule-based strategies earned more than simply holding the gold ETF. But 55 had smaller drawdowns, eleven had a better Sharpe ratio, and trend and volatility rules worked noticeably better on gold than on the S&P 500.

The test

Every strategy on Quant Arena runs under the same rules: daily data, signals at the close executed on the next session, walk-forward optimization that re-fits parameters on past data every six months, full allocation without leverage, and no trading costs unless stated. This study uses the 56 strategies that trade gold through GLD, the largest gold ETF, from 3 November 2021 to 3 November 2026. Buy-and-hold means owning GLD for the whole period.

Gold was hard to beat

Holding GLD returned 117% over the five years, 16.73% a year, with a Sharpe ratio of 0.91. Most of it came late: gold rose 27% in 2024 and 64% in 2025. Only three strategies earned a higher annual return, and none by more than a percentage point:

  • REG-01 Regime Switch: 17.42% a year, worst drawdown 17.7%
  • RISK-01 Drawdown Control: 17.12% a year, worst drawdown 24.4%
  • RISK-03 Stop Loss Overlay: 16.92% a year, worst drawdown 13.9%

It is the same story as on the S&P 500, where two of 62 strategies beat buy-and-hold over the same five years. In a strong, steady rally, any rule that spends time out of the market gives up return.

Where the strategies won: risk

55 of the 56 strategies had a smaller worst drawdown than holding gold, which fell 28.3% at its worst; the exception, MOM-03 52-Week High Momentum, fell 33.9%. Most of the difference comes from simply not always being invested: the median strategy was in the market 47% of the time. Eleven strategies also had a higher Sharpe ratio than buy-and-hold, meaning more return for each unit of volatility.

The best balance came from VOL-13 Volatility Regime Strategy: 11.4% a year with a Sharpe ratio of 1.20 and a worst drawdown of 10.5%, less than 40% of buy-and-hold's. REG-01 Regime Switch came closest to having both: 17.4% a year, Sharpe 1.19, worst drawdown 17.7%. RISK-03 Stop Loss Overlay matched gold's return with half its drawdown (13.9%).

Gold suits trend and volatility rules

All 56 strategies also ran on the S&P 500 (SPY) over the same five years, so they can be compared directly. 33 of the 56 had a higher Sharpe ratio on gold. By family, the median Sharpe ratio on gold versus SPY was:

  • Volatility strategies: 0.98 vs 0.47
  • Trend following: 0.78 vs 0.53
  • Momentum: 0.69 vs 0.63
  • Mean reversion: 0.35 vs 0.42

Gold's gains came in long runs, the kind of market that trend and volatility-breakout rules are built for. Buying dips did not help: mean-reversion rules did slightly worse on gold than on stocks.

The last twelve months: a 28% fall

The last year looked very different. Gold peaked on 29 January 2026, fell 28% to its low on 30 October, and ended the twelve months 2.6% lower. 41 of the 56 strategies did better than holding, and all but one had a smaller drawdown.

The one-year leader was MR-01 RSI Mean Reversion: +18.5% with a Sharpe ratio of 1.80, from 22 trades and only 9% of the time in the market. Over the full five years the same strategy made 4.8% a year on gold. Twenty-two trades is a small sample, and one-year leaders change often, so treat them with care.

How sure can we be?

On gold, 25 of the 56 strategies have a probabilistic Sharpe ratio above 95%: judged on its own, each has a 95% or higher probability that its true Sharpe ratio is above zero. On the S&P 500 the count is 11 of 62. But once you correct for testing hundreds of strategy-and-market pairs at the same time (the deflated Sharpe ratio), none reaches 95%; the best, VOL-13, gets to 80%.

That is the same lesson as in our luck-adjusted study of all 72 strategies: these are promising candidates, not proven edges.

Before costs

All figures are before trading costs. At an assumed 10 basis points per unit traded, the strategies above give up between 0.3 and 2 points of annual return: about 0.3 for RISK-01, 0.9 for REG-01, 1.6 for RISK-03 and 2 for VOL-13. Busier strategies pay much more: TREND-17 Chandelier Exit, with 332 trades, drops from 11.5% to roughly 4.8% a year. Every strategy page has a cost slider to try other levels.

What to take from it

  • If you just wanted gold's return, holding it was hard to beat: 53 of 56 strategies earned less over five years.
  • If you wanted gold with less pain, several rules cut the worst drawdown by between a third and almost two-thirds while keeping much of the return, and volatility- and regime-based rules did best.
  • One-year winners, and any result not adjusted for multiple testing, deserve extra skepticism.
