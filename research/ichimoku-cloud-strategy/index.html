Ichimoku Cloud vs a 50/200 Moving Average: Ten-Market Test

Before trading costs, the Ichimoku Cloud (TREND-10) as coded here had a higher full-period Sharpe ratio than the 50/200 Dual Moving Average (TREND-05) on six of ten markets and a shallower maximum drawdown on eight (mean −39.4% vs −50.4%), but it made 1,460 entries against 218.

What the code does

Ichimoku Kinko Hyo was devised by Goichi Hosoda in 1930s Japan and is normally drawn from daily highs, lows and closes. The version on this site uses closes only: every "high" and "low" in the formulas is replaced by the close. The Tenkan line is therefore the midpoint of the highest and lowest close of the last 9 days, the Kijun line the same over 26 days, and the two cloud edges are built from the Tenkan/Kijun midpoint and from the 52-day midpoint, both shifted 26 days forward.

The rule: go long when the close is above the top of the cloud and Tenkan is above Kijun. Go to cash when the close falls below the bottom of the cloud or Tenkan is no longer above Kijun. If the close is inside the cloud with Tenkan above Kijun, it keeps its previous position. The Chikou (lagging) line is computed but not used, and it is long only. The first 78 days are flat while the lines warm up.

The 9 and 26 are re-chosen every six months from a grid around them (Tenkan 7 to 11, Kijun 21 to 31), after an initial three years on the defaults. Results are before trading costs, for the last five years to 2026-11-11 and for the full period (2010-01-04 for most markets, 2014-09-17 for bitcoin, 2017-11-09 for ether).

Ten markets, two windows

Each line gives CAGR / Sharpe / maximum drawdown for the last five years, then the full period, and the share of days in the market (days with a non-zero return).

  • US stocks: 6.3% / 0.71 / −9.7%; full 6.1% / 0.68 / −15.1%. In the market 60% of days.
  • Gold: 12.2% / 0.90 / −13.9%; full 6.9% / 0.65 / −17.4%. 40%.
  • Silver: 11.6% / 0.57 / −36.8%; full 7.9% / 0.48 / −40.4%. 33%.
  • Oil: 7.1% / 0.39 / −42.0%; full 4.2% / 0.31 / −42.0%. 39%.
  • Natural gas: −15.9% / −0.31 / −73.9%; full −11.6% / −0.32 / −88.1%. 24%.
  • Broad commodities: 3.9% / 0.35 / −33.8%; full 2.5% / 0.29 / −33.8%. 40%.
  • Agriculture: 2.0% / 0.27 / −19.1%; full 1.8% / 0.26 / −21.9%. 35%.
  • EUR/USD: 0.9% / 0.22 / −8.8%; full −1.0% / −0.18 / −32.0%. 33%.
  • Bitcoin: 10.1% / 0.40 / −41.4%; full 60.0% / 1.11 / −48.5%. 41%.
  • Ether: 1.4% / 0.19 / −55.0%; full 40.1% / 0.79 / −55.0%. 34%.

Best and worst

Gold was the best market over five years (Sharpe 0.90, drawdown 13.9%) and bitcoin over the full period (1.11, at a 48.5% drawdown). Natural gas was the worst on both windows: −15.9% a year over five years and −11.6% with an 88.1% drawdown over the full period. Nine markets had a positive annual return over five years and eight over the full period.

The system was invested on a median 37% of days (range 24% to 60%). On US stocks, where a buy-and-hold investor is always in, it was in the market 60% of days with a 15.1% full-period drawdown.

The worst calendar year in each market

In each market's worst full calendar year the system lost less than the market in all ten, and lost money in nine. The market fell an average of 42.8% in those years; Ichimoku averaged −6.1%.

The cases: US stocks in 2022, market −18.2%, Ichimoku −5.9% (in the market on 26% of days). Gold in 2013 (−28.3%): −4.6%, in the market 9% of days. Silver in 2013 (−36.3%): −1.9%. Oil in 2020 (−67.8%): a gain of 3.8%. Bitcoin in 2018 (−73.6%): −26.0%, in the market 10% of days. Natural gas in 2023 (−64.0%) cost it 16.6%. Ether's 2018 (−82.4%) cost only 5.0%, but that year sits inside the first three years of the history, and the system needed 78 days of warm-up.

Against a 50/200 moving average

Dual Moving Average (TREND-05) goes long when the 50-day average is above the 200-day, and is in cash otherwise. It has no other filter in the code, despite its description, and no adjustable settings. It is far slower: 218 entries across ten markets against Ichimoku's 1,460, about 101.3 days per holding on average against 11.0. It was in the market on a median 48% of days (24% to 80%). On a given day the two agree on being in or out a median 62% of the time.

Sharpe was close: Ichimoku was higher on six of ten markets in both windows, with means of 0.37 vs 0.35 over five years and 0.41 vs 0.34 over the full period. Ichimoku's edge was drawdown: shallower on seven markets over five years and eight over the full period. On US stocks, the moving average earned more (9.6% vs 6.3% over five years, 9.2% vs 6.1% over the full period) with a deeper drawdown (−33.7% vs −15.1% over the full period). Both are long only.

In their worst calendar years, Ichimoku did better on six of ten markets (mean −6.1% vs −10.0%). The moving average stayed entirely in cash in three: natural gas in 2023, agriculture in 2015 and ether in 2018, though ether's case is partly the 200 days of data it needs before it can signal. In bitcoin's 2018 it lost 51.3% against Ichimoku's 26.0%.

Where it fails

Replacing highs and lows with closes changes the indicator, so these results should not be read as a test of Ichimoku as usually drawn on a candlestick chart. Natural gas and EUR/USD show what a market without a lasting trend does to it: 148 and 158 entries, and negative annual returns over the full period (−11.6% and −1.0%).

Costs matter here. The 1,460 entries are about 6.7 times the moving average's, and none of that is in the numbers. Bitcoin and ether histories are short, so their full-period figures rest on few cycles, and the first three years of each market run on default settings rather than walk-forward ones.

Takeaways

  • Coded on closes only, Ichimoku was a faster, shallower-drawdown relative of a 50/200 cross, not a different signal; both are long only.
  • It was in the market a median 37% of days and kept the worst calendar year to −6.1% on average, against −42.8% for the markets.
  • The simple 50/200 cross made about 15% as many entries and earned more on US stocks, at a deeper drawdown.
  • Natural gas and EUR/USD were the markets where neither system worked on the full period.
