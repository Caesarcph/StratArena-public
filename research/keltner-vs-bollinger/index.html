Keltner Breakout vs Bollinger Reversion: Ten Markets Compared

A Keltner Channel breakout and a Bollinger Band reversion rule were never in the market on the same day across ten markets (0 overlapping days out of 4,648 for the reversion rule). Over five years each had the better Sharpe ratio on five markets; over the full history the breakout was ahead on all ten.

Two bands, two opposite bets

Both strategies draw a volatility envelope around a 20-day average, and both are long-only on the site. They disagree about what a touch of the envelope means.

  • Keltner Channel Breakout (KC-01): the midline is a 20-day exponential average and the bands sit 2 times the ATR above and below it, where the ATR is a 20-day simple average of the true range. It goes long when the close is above the upper band and exits when the close drops below the midline.
  • Bollinger Band Reversion (MR-02): the midline is a 20-day simple average and the bands sit 2 standard deviations away. It buys when the close is at or below the lower band and the bandwidth is above its own 20-day average. It exits when the close returns to the midline, after 10 days, or if the close is more than 3% under the lower band.

Walk-forward selection retunes the windows: on US stocks the breakout used 6 distinct settings and the reversion rule 7, starting from the first test period in January 2013. All results below are before trading costs, for five years (from 2021-11-11 to 2026-11-11) and for each market's full history, which begins in 2010 for most markets, September 2014 for bitcoin and November 2017 for ether.

They never hold at the same time

Pooled across the ten markets, the reversion rule was long on 4,648 days and the breakout on 10,582. On none of them were both long. That follows from the rules: the breakout enters only above its upper band and leaves when the close falls under its midline, while the reversion rule enters only at the lower band of its own envelope. The consequence is that the correlation of their daily returns is zero for practical purposes: between −0.001 and 0.000 on every market, in both windows.

That is not diversification in the usual sense. Zero correlation here comes from taking turns, not from independent information. Days in market also differ: the breakout was invested a median 27% of days over five years (13% to 35% across markets), the reversion rule 10% (7% to 15%).

Five years: a clean five-five split

By Sharpe ratio the breakout won on US stocks (0.69 against 0.42), gold (0.60 against 0.18), silver (0.39 against −0.10), bitcoin (0.55 against −0.12) and ether (0.23 against 0.16). Its best case was bitcoin at 14.6% a year against 2.6% for holding, with a −32.9% maximum drawdown against −75.9%.

The reversion rule won on oil (0.74 against 0.26), natural gas (0.08 against −0.72), broad commodities (0.81 against 0.27), agriculture (0.50 against −0.12) and EUR/USD (0.28 against −0.36). On oil it made 8.9% a year with a −13.0% drawdown and was long 12% of days; on natural gas the breakout lost 24.4% a year with an −80.9% drawdown while the reversion rule broke even.

Neither beat buy-and-hold on return in more than three of ten markets. On US stocks, where holding made 11.9% a year with a Sharpe ratio of 0.74 and a −24.5% drawdown, the breakout made 4.5% (0.69, −8.5%) and the reversion rule 3.3% (0.42, −9.0%).

Full history: the breakout wins all ten

Over the full history the picture is lopsided. The breakout had the higher Sharpe ratio on all ten markets, median 0.39 against 0.11 for the reversion rule. It beat holding on return in eight markets, the reversion rule in three, and both had a shallower drawdown than holding on all ten.

The breakout's best results were bitcoin (53.8% a year, Sharpe 1.13, −45.5% drawdown, against 52.0%, 0.80 and −83.4% for holding) and ether (32.4%, 0.69 against 22.9%, 0.56). The reversion rule's best Sharpe was 0.35, on US stocks, where it made 2.8% a year and the breakout 3.1% with a Sharpe of 0.51. The breakout had a negative return on three markets (oil, natural gas, EUR/USD) and the reversion rule on four (silver, oil, natural gas, EUR/USD).

Counting calendar years, with each market's first partial year skipped, the breakout beat the reversion rule in 86 of 149 market-years, the reversion rule in 63.

A bad stretch: 2022

In 2022 the split came back: the breakout was ahead in five markets and the reversion rule in five; the breakout finished positive on five and the reversion rule on six. On US stocks, down 18.2%, the breakout lost 4.1% and the reversion rule gained 5.7%. On natural gas the reversion rule gained 19.8% and the breakout lost 24.2%. On ether, down 67.5%, the breakout made 11.0% and the reversion rule lost 32.3%.

What a 50/50 mix did

Because the two never overlap, a daily-rebalanced 50/50 mix is simply half the capital working in each. It rarely beat both parents. Over five years the mix had a Sharpe ratio above both strategies on only three markets (US stocks, gold, ether) and above the weaker one on all ten. Over the full history it beat both on three (US stocks, broad commodities, agriculture), and its maximum drawdown was shallower than both on nine of ten.

The best case was US stocks over five years: 4.1% a year, Sharpe 0.75 and a −5.8% drawdown, against 4.5%, 0.69, −8.5% for the breakout and 3.3%, 0.42, −9.0% for the reversion rule. The cost shows up on markets where one side dominated: on bitcoin over the full history the mix made 28.5% a year at a Sharpe of 1.00 and a −40.2% drawdown, against 53.8%, 1.13 and −45.5% for the breakout alone.

How sure can we be?

Not very. None of the 20 five-year results (two strategies, ten markets) has a deflated Sharpe ratio above 95%, and none of the 20 full-history results does either. The highest are the breakout on US stocks over five years (52.9%) and on bitcoin over the full history (82.6%). The 90% bootstrap range for the breakout's five-year return on US stocks is −0.2% to 9.7% a year, and for the reversion rule −1.0% to 7.8%; on bitcoin the breakout's range is −6.9% to 40.6%. Windows are short and few markets are independent, so read the split as a pattern, not a proof.

Takeaways

  • The two rules are mirror images that never trade on the same day; their zero correlation is mechanical.
  • Over five years each won five markets by Sharpe ratio; over the full history the breakout won all ten.
  • A 50/50 mix beat both parents on Sharpe in only three markets per window, and had a shallower drawdown than both on nine of ten markets over the full history but only three over five years.
  • Neither reliably beat buy-and-hold on return; the breakout's edge in this sample is a shallower drawdown than holding (all ten markets over the full history) and large gains on crypto.
  • Over five years reversion looked best where the breakout did worst (oil, natural gas, agriculture), so the market matters as much as the band.
