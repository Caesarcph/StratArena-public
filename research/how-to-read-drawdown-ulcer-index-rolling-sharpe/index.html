Same Max Drawdown, Different Pain: Ulcer Index and Rolling Sharpe

On US stocks over the last five years, Z-Score Mean Reversion and Volatility Breakout both had a maximum drawdown of about 10% (−10.4% and −10.1%), yet one spent at most 224 days below its previous high and the other 1,006, and their Ulcer indexes were 1.3% and 5.4%.

Four numbers, four different questions

Most strategy tables lead with one risk number, the maximum drawdown. This guide uses real pairs of strategies from the arena to show what that number misses and what to read next to it. All figures are from backtests before trading costs, over the five-year window that starts on 2021-11-11 and ends on 2026-11-11.

  • Maximum drawdown: the deepest fall from a previous peak to a later low.
  • Time under water: the longest stretch, in calendar days, spent below a previous peak. The site also shows the share of days spent below one.
  • Ulcer Index: the root mean square of the daily drawdown from the running peak, so it grows with both depth and duration.
  • Rolling Sharpe: the Sharpe ratio recomputed over a moving 252-day window, so you see how it changed instead of one average.

Maximum drawdown: depth and nothing else

Maximum drawdown is one episode. It says how far the worst fall went, not how long it lasted, how often the strategy was below a high, or whether it recovered. Buy-and-hold SPY had a −24.5% maximum drawdown in this window and a Sharpe ratio of 0.74.

Take two strategies on SPY. Z-Score Mean Reversion (MR-06) had a maximum drawdown of −10.4% and Volatility Breakout (VOL-01) −10.1%. Of 53 strategies with meaningful trading on SPY, they ranked 11th and 10th by drawdown: next to each other on any leaderboard. They also spent almost the same share of time in the market, 14.0% of days against 14.4%. So neither depth nor exposure separates them.

Time under water: 224 days against 1,006

The dates do. Z-Score Mean Reversion peaked on 2025-02-28, fell to its low on 2025-04-08 (39 days) and made a new high on 2025-10-13. Its longest stretch below a previous peak ran from 2025-03-03 to 2025-10-13: 224 days. Over the whole window it was below a previous high on 29% of days.

Volatility Breakout peaked on 2021-11-16, hit its low on 2022-06-27 (223 days later) and did not make a new high until 2024-08-19. That stretch, from 2021-11-17 to 2024-08-19, lasted 1,006 days, almost three years, and it was below a previous high on 99% of all days. The first fall was the same size; the second kept it below a high 4.5 times as long. Annual return followed: 8.0% against 1.1%.

Being underwater most of the time is normal here: the median SPY strategy was below a previous high on 91% of days, and 36 of 53 were below one on more than 90% of days. Against that, 29% is unusual.

The Ulcer Index puts depth and duration in one number

For each day, take the percentage the equity is below its running peak, square it, average over the window and take the square root. A day 10% down counts four times as much as a day 5% down, and a long stretch counts for every day of it. That is why a strategy that hovers 3% to 5% below its high for years can score worse than one that dropped 10% once and recovered.

Here the Ulcer Index was 1.3% for Z-Score Mean Reversion and 5.4% for Volatility Breakout: 4.2 times higher for the same maximum drawdown. The ranks tell the same story: by drawdown they were 11th and 10th of 53, by Ulcer Index 2nd and 22nd.

The two measures usually agree: the rank correlation of maximum drawdown and Ulcer Index across the 53 strategies is 0.87. Large gaps are the exception, not the rule. Among strategy pairs on the same market and window whose maximum drawdowns were within one percentage point of each other (790 pairs over five years), 33 had Ulcer Indexes at least twice apart and 157 had longest stretches below a high at least twice apart.

Two more pairs

Gold: CCI Mean Reversion (MR-05) and Regime Switch (REG-01, long while the price is above its 200-day average and 60-day volatility is under 25%) had maximum drawdowns of −17.0% and −17.7%. Regime Switch made 16.8% a year against 4.5%, and still had the higher Ulcer Index (9.2% against 3.5%): its fall ran from 2022-03-08 to 2022-12-22 and it needed until 2024-04-03 to recover, 756 days under water. Return and Ulcer Index measure different things, and a good number on the first does not buy the second.

Oil: Stochastic Oscillator Reversion (MR-10) and Trend + Mean Reversion Hybrid (COMBO-04) had maximum drawdowns of −28.7% and −27.8%, both in the same 28 days, from 2026-06-03 to 2026-07-01. MR-10 had recovered by 2026-10-01. The Hybrid had been below a previous high for 1,435 days (2022-06-29 to 2026-06-03) before that fall and is still below its peak on 2026-11-11. Ulcer Index: 5.9% against 14.1%; annual return: 21.6% against −2.1%.

Rolling Sharpe: one ratio, many periods

A Sharpe ratio averages over a whole window, and the window decides. On SPY, Volatility Breakout's Sharpe ratio was 1.31 over one year, 0.76 over three, 0.21 over five and 0.09 over its full history; Z-Score Mean Reversion's was 1.53, 0.84, 0.89 and 0.44. Judged on one year alone the two look alike. The rolling 252-day Sharpe shows why they are not: Z-Score Mean Reversion's stayed between 0.01 and 1.89 and was positive on every day; Volatility Breakout's ranged from −1.52 (2025-11-13) to 2.69 (2026-10-15) and was positive on 59% of days.

Rolling Sharpe also catches a slump the average hides. CCI Mean Reversion on gold has a five-year Sharpe of 0.52 but a one-year Sharpe of −0.72: its rolling value was at its low of −0.83 on 2026-11-03, the day its open drawdown bottomed.

What these measures cannot tell you

Every figure depends on its window. The five-year window opens on 2021-11-11, so a drawdown can start in its first days (Volatility Breakout's peak is on 2021-11-16), and a drawdown that is still open (CCI Mean Reversion on gold, the Hybrid on oil) counts as unfinished. All of it is backtested, before trading costs, with walk-forward parameters. A single max drawdown or rolling Sharpe is also one path out of many: on the site the 90% bootstrap range shows how much deeper the worst fall could have been.

A short checklist

  • Read maximum drawdown together with the longest stretch below a high and the Ulcer Index.
  • When two strategies have similar drawdowns, compare their Ulcer Indexes: a ratio of 2 or more means a different experience.
  • A Sharpe ratio that is similar over one, three and five years says more than one with a single good window.
  • Look at the rolling Sharpe chart for the last year before trusting the long-run number.
  • Ask how long you could watch a strategy sit below its high, and compare that with its longest stretch.
