Which Strategies Survived 2022, and Did the Winners Keep Winning?

In calendar 2022 US stocks fell 18.2% and only 10 of 55 single-market strategies finished positive, 8 of them mean-reversion rules. In 2023 those ten all stayed positive, yet their median return was 3.7% against 14.8% for the rest, and the rank correlation between the two years was −0.58.

The question and how it was tested

This article asks which of the site's rule-based strategies came through it with a gain, what they had in common, and whether being a 2022 winner told you anything about 2023.

The test is calendar 2022: the return from the last close of 2021 to the last close of 2022, for 55 single-market strategies on each of ten markets, 550 strategy-market series in all. Divergence Strategy (COMBO-03) is left out because its code looks ahead. The 16 portfolio strategies, which trade baskets, are counted separately. Parameters come from walk-forward selection, positions are decided at the close and earn the next day's return, and everything is before trading costs. Data runs to 2026-11-11.

US stocks: ten of 55 finished positive

Buy-and-hold SPY lost 18.2% in 2022, with a maximum drawdown inside the year of −24.5%. Of the 55 strategies, 10 finished positive, 2 finished flat with no trades (Elder Triple Screen, TREND-19, and TRIX Reversion, MR-13) and 43 lost money. The median was −6.4%. Fifty-one lost less than holding; three lost more, and a fourth, Stop Loss Overlay, matched it to two decimals (−18.18%).

The ten winners were RSI Mean Reversion (MR-01, +11.2%), Ultimate Oscillator (MR-15, +10.4%), Linear Regression Channel (TREND-14, +9.4%), Kalman Filter Mean Reversion (MR-03, +6.2%), Stochastic Oscillator Reversion (MR-10, +5.7%), Bollinger Band Reversion (MR-02, +5.7%), Z-Score Mean Reversion (MR-06, +4.7%), Hurst-Filtered Mean Reversion (MR-07, +4.5%), Detrended Price Oscillator (MR-14, +1.7%) and MACD + RSI Filter (COMBO-02, +1.1%).

The worst were Drawdown Control (RISK-01, −24.3%), Parabolic SAR (SAR-01, −19.5%), Time-Series Momentum (MOM-01, −18.5%) and Stop Loss Overlay (RISK-03, −18.2%), the first three below holding and the fourth level with it, followed by HMA Crossover (HMA-01, −17.7%) and Williams %R Reversion (MR-11, −17.4%).

Which families held up

Using the strategy tags (a strategy can carry several):

  • Mean Reversion: 15 strategies, median +1.7%, 8 positive. The six that lost money included Williams %R Reversion (−17.4%) and CCI Mean Reversion (MR-05, −10.3%), so the tag is no guarantee.
  • Trend: 24 strategies, median −8.0%, 1 positive (Linear Regression Channel).
  • Momentum: 12 strategies, median −10.8%, 1 positive (MACD + RSI Filter).
  • Volatility: 9 strategies, median −8.0%, 1 positive (Bollinger Band Reversion).
  • Regime: 4 strategies, median −12.7%, none positive. Risk Management: 4 strategies, median −16.0%, none positive.

Eight of the ten winners carry the Mean Reversion tag. A note on what these rules do: RSI Mean Reversion is long only on days when its 14-day RSI is below 30 and flat otherwise (it does not hold until RSI recovers), which is why it was in the market on just 13% of days.

What the survivors had in common

They were rarely invested and shallow in their losses. The ten winners were in the market a median 25% of days in 2022 against 36% for the 43 losers, and nine of ten were under 50%. Their worst drawdown inside the year had a median of −8.2% (range −5.5% to −13.7%), against −13.3% for the losers and −24.5% for SPY. Across all 55 strategies the rank correlation between 2022 return and days in the market was −0.42.

Being out of the market was not enough. Twenty strategies were invested under 25% of days and only five of them finished positive; their median return was −3.1%. The one winner that was heavily invested, Linear Regression Channel, was in the market 65% of days, and its code lets it go short: it was short on 33% of days in 2022. It is the only one of the 55 US-stock strategies besides Crisis Alpha (TAIL-01) that took short positions that year.

Other markets: 2022 was a commodity year

Across all ten markets 231 of 550 series finished positive. The split follows the market, not the rule:

  • Where holding rose (oil +29.0%, broad commodities +19.3%, natural gas +12.9%, agriculture +2.5%, silver +2.4%), 168 of 275 series were positive.
  • Where holding fell (US stocks −18.2%, EUR/USD −5.9%, gold −0.8%, bitcoin −64.3%, ether −67.5%), only 63 of 275 were.

Bitcoin was the starkest case: all 55 strategies lost less than holding, but only four finished positive, while on ether 11 did. No single family dominated outside US stocks: mean reversion was positive in 63 of 150 series (median −0.9%) and trend in 100 of 240 (median −1.0%). Of the 16 portfolio strategies, 6 finished positive, led by Dual Momentum (MOM-02, +29.0%), Hierarchical Risk Parity (PORT-05, +20.4%) and FX Carry (CARRY-01, +20.2%).

Did 2022's winners keep winning in 2023?

They did not, on any measure we tried. SPY rose 26.2% in 2023 and 51 of the 55 US-stock strategies were positive. All ten 2022 winners were positive again, but none beat holding, and their median return was 3.7% against 14.8% for the other 45. The ten worst of 2022 returned a median 17.1% in 2023. Only one of the 2022 top ten finished in the top half of the 2023 ranking, and their median 2023 rank was 45 out of 55. The Spearman rank correlation between the two years was −0.58.

The other nine markets are less clear-cut but lean the same way. Across all ten markets the correlation was positive on only three, ranging from −0.58 to 0.21. Pooling all markets, 103 of the 231 strategies that made money in 2022 were positive in 2023 (median −0.4%), against 207 of the 319 others (median +4.1%).

What this does not show

One year is one observation per strategy, and market conditions produced much of it: the same rules scored very differently across markets. The negative correlation is a statement about these two years, not a law. Samples are small in places (only four winners on bitcoin), the winners were in the market a median 25% of days, so each result rests on a limited number of trading days, and none of this includes trading costs.

Takeaways

  • In calendar 2022, 10 of 55 US-stock strategies made money; 8 were mean-reversion rules that were in the market a quarter of the time.
  • Low exposure and shallow drawdowns marked the survivors, but only 5 of the 20 strategies invested under a quarter of the days finished positive.
  • Across markets the result depended on the market: 168 of 275 series were positive where holding rose, 63 of 275 where it fell.
  • 2022's winners were mostly 2023's laggards: the rank correlation was −0.58 on US stocks and positive on only three of ten markets.
