Parabolic SAR vs Supertrend: Which Trailing Stop Did Better?

Parabolic SAR (SAR-01) earned a positive annual return on nine of ten markets over its full history, before trading costs, with EUR/USD the best at a 1.17 Sharpe ratio. But its maximum drawdown was deeper than Supertrend's (SUPER-01) on eight of ten markets.

Parabolic SARSupertrendTrailing StopTrend

What the rule does

Parabolic SAR (SAR-01) keeps a stop below price that creeps upward every day. It starts with an acceleration factor of 0.02, adds 0.02 each time the trend makes a new high, and caps it at 0.20, so the stop moves faster the longer a trend runs. When the day's low touches the stop, the system flips.

On this site it is long only: an up-trend means fully invested and a down-trend means cash. It reads each day's high and low, decides at the close, and earns the following day's return. Everything here is before trading costs. The last five years run to 2026-11-11; the full period starts on 2010-01-04 for most markets, 2014-09-17 for bitcoin and 2017-11-09 for ether.

Why it might work

The idea is that the longer a move runs, the closer the stop sits to price, so a profit is locked in before a reversal can take it all back. The price is paid in sideways markets, where the stop keeps getting touched. Across the ten markets the system made 3,491 entries since 2010, against 1,973 for Supertrend, and it stayed in the market on a median of 51% of days (range 43% to 62%).

The first three years of each market use the default settings above. After that the parameters are re-picked every six months from a small grid around them.

Best and worst

EUR/USD was the best market on both windows, and the only one where the five-year maximum drawdown stayed in single digits. Natural gas was the worst on both: a 96.2% maximum drawdown over the full period. Seven markets had a positive annual return over the last five years and nine over the full period.

The crypto numbers show how much the window matters. Bitcoin and ether made 40.0% and 42.5% a year over their full histories but lost money over the last five years. A drawdown of 71.4% on bitcoin is what that full-period figure cost.

The worst calendar year in each market

Taking each market's worst full calendar year, SAR lost less than the market in nine of ten and lost money in nine of ten. The exception was US stocks in 2022: the market fell 18.2% and the system fell 19.5%, being in the market on 49% of days that year.

The best cases were deep one-way falls. In oil's 2020 (the market fell 67.8%) the system gained 9.4%; in silver's 2013 (−36.3%) it lost 6.7%; in gold's 2013 (−28.3%) it lost 12.6%; in bitcoin's 2018 (−73.6%) it lost 14.7%. In natural gas's 2023 (−64.0%) it still lost 50.3%: a stop that is touched often cannot protect a market that keeps bouncing.

Against Supertrend

Supertrend (SUPER-01) is the closest relative: also long only, also a flipping stop, but built from a smoothed average true range (EMA span 10, multiplier 2.5) around the day's midpoint, and it flips only when the close crosses the band. On a given day the two agree on whether to hold or not on a median 78% of days.

Sharpe is close to a coin flip: SAR was higher on five markets over five years and on four over the full period, although its mean Sharpe was higher in both (0.34 vs 0.25, and 0.49 vs 0.38), pulled up by EUR/USD and gold. Drawdown is where Supertrend wins: shallower on nine of ten markets in five years and eight of ten in the full period (mean −36.7% vs −43.2% over five years).

In those same years Supertrend did better than the market in all ten. On US stocks in 2022 it fell 5.1% against SAR's 19.5%. But SAR did better in only four of ten of those years, and the two disagreed sharply on ether in 2018, which was inside the default-settings period: Supertrend gained 47.8% in the year the market fell 82.4%, including 30.3% in January alone.

Where it fails

Whipsaw is the cost of a close stop. With 3,491 entries, even small trading costs would take a bite that this backtest does not show. The numbers also cannot say that a good full-period result will repeat; they mix very different stretches, and the 2018 ether gain above includes a single 30.3% month.

Both systems here are long only, so none of the results say anything about shorting a down-trend. Natural gas shows what that leaves: it was out of the market on more than half the days, and still had a 96.2% maximum drawdown.

Takeaways

  • SAR is a faster, more active stop than Supertrend: more entries, deeper drawdowns, similar Sharpe.
  • On the ten markets, EUR/USD and gold suited it best; natural gas, bitcoin and ether over five years did not.
  • It softened most calendar-year falls (nine of ten), but not by enough to avoid losses (nine of ten still lost).
  • Backtested, before trading costs; the first three years of each market use default settings, not walk-forward choices.
