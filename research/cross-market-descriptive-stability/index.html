Do the same strategies behave alike across markets?

A descriptive stability study of six frozen pilots on SPY, GLD and BTC-USD, with separate benchmarks and no pooled winner.

ReproducibilityCostsFixed sample

Question: what stays stable across these three markets?

We ask whether each fixed strategy retains its net-return sign and matched-benchmark excess sign across SPY, GLD and BTC-USD at the same fee. The hypothesis of market-invariant behavior is descriptive and deliberately weak: a sign change contradicts it within this sample, while matching signs cannot establish general stability. The markets share a calendar span but have different observations, return distributions and benchmarks.

All six pilots remain in fixed order. No strategy was chosen for looking stable. Every fee is shown in the cost article and packet; the 25-bp panel below is the fixed high-fee endpoint, not an optimized setting. Rank always belongs to that market’s separate 56-member matched cohort, never to a pooled multi-market leaderboard.

At 25 bp, RISK-04 has positive net return on GLD but negative net return on SPY and BTC-USD. MR-16 is negative on all three, and TREND-14 was already gross-negative on all three. MR-01, TURTLE-01 and RP-01 remain net-positive on all three; of those endpoint comparisons, only RP-01/BTC-USD has positive matched-benchmark excess. A positive strategy return therefore does not imply benchmark outperformance.

The stable negative outcomes are as informative as the positive ones. They challenge any universal claim based on strategy labels. The cross-market sign patterns and rank changes are observations, not independent statistical trials: correlated markets, a single historical window and six selected pilots do not provide a generalization test.

Fixed evidence, rules and reproduction

The six original pilots are MR-01 (RSI Mean Reversion), MR-16 (Connors RSI), TURTLE-01 (Turtle Trading), TREND-14 (Linear Regression Channel), RP-01 (Volatility Target) and RISK-04 (Trailing Stop Strategy). RP-01 is not the separate risk-parity basket. All 18 strategy-market pairs and 72 fee scenarios are retained. This retrospective selection was fixed before table extraction; it is not a prospective preregistration.

The cost and cross-market panels use 2021-11-05 through 2026-11-05, with 1,255 closes for SPY/GLD and 1,827 for BTC-USD. Return intervals are one fewer. The calendar audit uses each run’s full native dates. Daily site updates do not replace this frozen evidence.

It does not independently recheck the calendar audit outcomes or their old/new input identities, and does not regenerate decisions from fresh vendor inputs. Failure conditions and interpretation

Fees use drift-adjusted one-way risky turnover and are charged after each gross return: (1 + grossReturn) × (1 − turnover × bps / 10000). Strategy and matched benchmark are charged separately. The start-close anchor excludes fees already paid at the first included close. Fees do not feed back into targets or drift decisions. Paid fees are start-close capital units, not terminal wealth drag.

Financing, borrow, funding, tax, impact, broker fills and forced terminal liquidation are excluded. These are separate strategy paths, not a pooled or independent-sleeve portfolio. Reject comparisons with changed dates, input, rule, model, anchor or fee units. Historical outcomes do not establish future returns or causal strategy-family superiority. MT5 parity remains deferred.
