60/40, All Weather, Dual Momentum: 22 Famous Rules Replicated

Replicated on the site's markets since 2010, the best return per unit of drawdown among the 22 famous portfolios and rules came from a Stage 2 trend rule (0.89), but without its bitcoin slot it falls to 0.39. The fixed-weight 60/40-style, All Weather-style and 90/10 mixes all landed at 0.41 to 0.42, the same as holding SPY.

The question

Famous portfolios are usually quoted with their original ingredients: bonds, futures, stock selection, leverage. The site only trades ten markets (SPY, gold, silver, oil, natural gas, broad commodities, agriculture, EUR/USD, bitcoin, ether) and has no bond market. So what do these portfolios look like once they are rebuilt from what is available?

The site's data file defines 22 of them. Each is run as a fixed-weight allocation or as a daily rule on SPY or on several markets, before trading costs, with positions decided at the close earning the next day's return. The fixed-weight mixes built on SPY start on 4 January 2010 (16.9 years, 4,241 days); the rule sets start when their first signal is valid, from 2010 to April 2015. Results run to 11 November 2026.

How each was adapted

The site labels every replication. Five are "full": Connors RSI(2) (the site's RSI(2) pullback rule on SPY), a 100% SPY buy-and-hold, bitcoin buy-and-hold, the Halloween rule (SPY from the end of October to the end of April, cash otherwise) and a turn-of-the-month rule. Fourteen are "adapted": 60/40, All Weather, Permanent Portfolio, Golden Butterfly, Buffett 90/10, Faber's 10-month rule, a one-leg dual momentum, both Turtle systems, O'Neil's new-high rule, Weinstein's Stage 2, Clenow's trend rule, 12-month time-series momentum and 10% volatility targeting. Three are "interpretation", meaning the site's own reading of a loosely specified method: Livermore, a 200-day average attributed to Paul Tudor Jones, and the Mayer multiple on bitcoin.

All descriptions are as defined on the site. Two kinds of adaptation matter most. First, bonds become cash earning 0%: 40% of the 60/40 mix, 55% of All Weather, 50% of Permanent Portfolio, 40% of Golden Butterfly and 10% of Buffett 90/10. Second, futures strategies are rebuilt on ten ETF, FX and crypto markets with long-only positions and a fixed slot per market, instead of volatility-based sizing.

The fixed-weight mixes

Each is rebalanced at the last close of every year; figures are annualised return (CAGR), volatility, maximum drawdown and worst calendar year, 2010 to 2026, with 16 full years:

  • 60/40-style (60% SPY, 40% cash): 8.5%, 10.1%, -20.6%, -10.9%.
  • All Weather-style (30% SPY, 7.5% gold, 7.5% broad commodities, 55% cash): 5.3%, 5.9%, -12.5%, -4.1%.
  • Permanent Portfolio-style (25% SPY, 25% gold, 50% cash): 5.8%, 6.3%, -10.3%, -4.7%.
  • Golden Butterfly-style (40% SPY, 20% gold, 40% cash): 7.5%, 7.7%, -14.6%, -7.4%.
  • Buffett 90/10-style (90% SPY, 10% cash): 12.6%, 15.3%, -30.5%, -16.4%.
  • SPY buy-and-hold: 13.9%, 17.0%, -33.7%, -18.2%.

In every case the worst year was 2022. These are dilutions of SPY with cash and gold, so smaller drawdowns mostly reflect how much of the portfolio sat in cash.

The rule sets

Rules on one market: the one-leg dual momentum (SPY or cash on a positive 12-month return, from January 2011) returned 10.8% with a -33.7% drawdown, the same as SPY, so it did not avoid the 2020 crash. The 200-day average rule returned 10.6% with a -19.8% drawdown and a -14.9% worst year. The 10% volatility target returned 9.6% with -12.8%. Connors RSI(2) was invested only 11.9% of the time on average and returned 4.3% (-14.7%). The Halloween rule returned 7.8% (-33.7%) and the turn-of-the-month rule 3.3% (-13.8%).

Rules on several markets: Weinstein's Stage 2 on six markets returned 13.8% (-15.5%, worst year -11.1%); Turtle System 1 and 2 returned 8.3% and 8.1% (-15.9% and -13.5%); Clenow's trend rule 5.5% (-8.9%, worst year -2.0%), investing on average just 14.6%; O'Neil's rule 10.1% (-26.5%); 12-month time-series momentum 8.6% (-21.4%); Faber's 10-month rule 5.9% (-16.3%); the Livermore interpretation 5.7% (-23.7%). Bitcoin buy-and-hold returned 52.0% with a -83.4% drawdown over 12.2 years, and the Mayer-multiple rule 63.0% with -78.7%.

Best return per unit of drawdown

Dividing CAGR by the absolute maximum drawdown, SPY scores 0.41. On each portfolio's own history the top five were Weinstein's Stage 2 (0.89), the Mayer multiple (0.80, 11.6 years of bitcoin only), the volatility target (0.75), bitcoin buy-and-hold (0.62) and Clenow's rule (0.62). At the bottom were the Halloween rule (0.23), turn-of-the-month (0.24) and the Livermore interpretation (0.24).

Because start dates differ, I also ran the 20 portfolios that do not rely on bitcoin alone from a common start of 31 January 2011 (15.8 years). Weinstein's Stage 2 still led (0.84), then the volatility target (0.72), Clenow's rule and Turtle System 2 (0.62 each), Turtle System 1 and Permanent Portfolio (0.55 each), the 200-day average (0.52) and Golden Butterfly (0.50). 60/40, All Weather and Buffett 90/10 scored 0.41, level with SPY. Of the 20 (SPY itself is one), 11 beat SPY on this measure, and none of the other 19 beat its CAGR. By label, the median score was 0.26 for the 4 "full" portfolios, 0.46 for the 14 "adapted" and 0.37 for the 2 "interpretation" ones, too few in each group to read a pattern.

The part that depends on crypto

Rules on ten markets include bitcoin and ether, which only entered the data in September 2014 and November 2017. Holding the crypto slots in cash and keeping everything else identical gives:

  • Weinstein's Stage 2 (1 crypto slot of 6): CAGR 13.8% falls to 5.8%, and return per drawdown from 0.89 to 0.39.
  • Turtle System 1 (2 of 10): 8.3% to 1.4%; Turtle System 2: 8.1% to 2.4%.
  • Clenow's rule: 5.5% to 1.5%. O'Neil's rule: 10.1% to 3.0%. 12-month momentum: 8.6% to 2.3%. Livermore interpretation: 5.7% to 1.0%.

So the high scores of the multi-market rules are largely a bitcoin and ether result over a sample that contains a few very large moves. Without them, the best of these scored 0.39.

What the adaptations mean for comparing with the originals

Replacing bonds with cash changes the character of a portfolio, so the figures for 60/40, All Weather and Permanent Portfolio are not the originals' figures, and the originals' bond income and their behaviour when stocks and bonds diverge are missing. The All Weather-style mix here is also unlevered. The 0.41 to 0.42 scores for 60/40, All Weather and Buffett 90/10 say only that diluting SPY with cash scaled return and drawdown together, not that those portfolios are equal. Gold lifted Permanent Portfolio (0.56) and Golden Butterfly (0.51) above that.

Rule sets lose stock selection, shorting, futures breadth and volatility sizing, so Turtle, Clenow and O'Neil here are simplified relatives. The "interpretation" ones are the site's reading, not a published rule. The site's definitions were compiled from general knowledge and are used as given, not checked against the original sources.

Takeaways

  • Return per unit of drawdown (CAGR divided by maximum drawdown): SPY 0.41; 60/40-style 0.41; All Weather-style 0.42; Permanent-style 0.56; the best multi-market rule 0.89, falling to 0.39 without crypto.
  • Over the common 15.8 years, none of the other 19 portfolios beat SPY's 13.9% CAGR; the smoother ones gave up return for smaller drawdowns.
  • Bond-to-cash substitution makes the allocation results mostly SPY-with-cash and gold; read them as such.
  • Dual momentum here is one leg only and matched SPY's -33.7% maximum drawdown.
  • Labels matter: 5 full, 14 adapted, 3 interpretation. Most of the 22 are simplified relatives of the original.
