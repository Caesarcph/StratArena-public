What Follows High Volatility? 21-Day Returns in 10 Markets

After high-volatility days, the next 21 days beat the market's own average in 5 of 10 markets and lagged it in the other 5, and no gap exceeds two standard errors. The one consistent pattern is wider outcomes: 9 of 10 markets had a larger spread of 21-day returns after high volatility.

The question

The site's market regime dashboard labels each market every day as steady up, volatile up, mixed, down or stress, and it shows what happened over the following month. A natural reading is that high volatility is a warning, or alternatively a buying opportunity. This article asks what the stored history says for each of the ten markets, and where it says nothing.

Two cautions first. These are descriptive averages over overlapping windows, not forecasts, and for each market the number of independent observations is far smaller than the number of days. All results are from daily closes, with no trading costs involved.

How regimes and outcomes are defined

The rules come from the dashboard code and use closes up to that day only. A day is "up" when the close is above its 200-day average and the 50-day average is above the 200-day, "down" when both are below, and "mixed" otherwise. Volatility is the standard deviation of the last 20 daily returns, annualised, ranked against the previous 756 observations (about three years of trading days) of the same market. At the 80th percentile or above it counts as high. That gives five regimes: steady up (up, not high), volatile up (up, high), mixed, down (down, not high) and stress (down, high).

The outcome is the return from that day's close to the close 21 observations later. The first classified day needs 200 days of prices and a year of volatility history. Classified history therefore runs from 31 January 2011 for US stocks, gold, silver, oil, natural gas, broad commodities and agriculture (3,970 days, 15.8 years), from 17 January 2011 for EUR/USD (4,118 days), from 15 June 2015 for bitcoin (4,168 days, 11.4 years) and from 7 August 2018 for ether (3,019 days, 8.3 years). Bitcoin and ether trade every day, so their 21 observations are three calendar weeks.

US stocks, regime by regime

US stocks are the clearest example. The average 21-day return over all classified days was +1.2% (median +1.6%, 68.4% of windows positive). By regime, with days in the regime and episodes:

  • Steady up: 2,923 days, 28 episodes; mean +1.0%, median +1.5%, 68.8% up.
  • Volatile up: 238 days, 31 episodes; mean +0.6%, median +1.6%, 64.8% up.
  • Mixed: 373 days, 46 episodes; mean +1.5%, median +2.0%, 62.7% up.
  • Down: 109 days, 21 episodes; mean +0.3%, median +2.6%, 56.0% up.
  • Stress: 327 days, 19 episodes; mean +3.5%, median +4.3%, 78.0% up.

Stress, the high-volatility downtrend, was followed by the best month on average. But 327 days in 19 episodes is only about 15.6 non-overlapping 21-day windows, and the spread was wide (standard deviation 4.9%). The "down" regime has only 109 days and its mean and median disagree, so it supports no reading.

High volatility against the average, ten markets

Pooling the two high-volatility regimes (volatile up and stress) gives a larger sample. For each market, high-volatility days that had a 21-day outcome, the number of episodes (the two regimes counted separately), the mean outcome and its gap to the all-days mean:

  • US stocks: 560 days, 50 episodes; +2.3% vs +1.2%, gap +1.11 points.
  • Gold: 864 days, 63 episodes; +0.5% vs +0.7%, gap -0.15.
  • Silver: 792 days, 56 episodes; +1.9% vs +0.7%, gap +1.21.
  • Oil: 855 days, 48 episodes; +1.0% vs +0.3%, gap +0.73.
  • Natural gas: 802 days, 61 episodes; -5.2% vs -1.3%, gap -3.90.
  • Broad commodities: 836 days, 65 episodes; +1.5% vs +0.3%, gap +1.22.
  • Agriculture: 617 days, 62 episodes; -0.5% vs 0.0%, gap -0.56.
  • EUR/USD: 649 days, 57 episodes; -0.5% vs -0.1%, gap -0.39.
  • Bitcoin: 623 days, 47 episodes; +8.4% vs +4.4%, gap +3.98.
  • Ether: 448 days, 39 episodes; +0.7% vs +3.6%, gap -2.85.

Five gaps are positive and five negative.

Where the pattern is real enough to mention

To judge size, I compared each gap with a standard error built from the market's 21-day return spread and the number of independent 21-day blocks (high-volatility days divided by 21). No market reaches a ratio of 2. The largest in absolute terms are natural gas (-1.80), broad commodities (+1.50), US stocks (+1.36) and bitcoin (+1.19). Natural gas is the clearest case of high volatility being followed by worse returns: -5.2% with 32.7% of windows positive against 42.6% for all days, with the same sign in both halves of the sample and in the median (-6.9% against -2.0%). Its stress regime alone (570 days, 42 episodes) averaged -5.5%.

The pattern that does repeat is dispersion. After high volatility, the standard deviation of 21-day returns was larger than the all-days figure in 9 of 10 markets: US stocks 5.1% against 4.2%, silver 13.6% against 9.4%, oil 15.4% against 10.9%, bitcoin 24.4% against 18.2%. Ether was the exception (18.7% against 21.5%). Whatever the average return, a wider range of outcomes followed.

In EUR/USD and agriculture, the stress regime was followed by a lower share of positive months (37.0% and 37.3%, against 48.3% and 48.0% for all days), with 513 and 276 days; the direction is consistent but the gaps are small.

Where the sample is too small or the pattern too weak

Of the 50 market-and-regime cells, 5 have fewer than 200 days: US stocks "down" (109 days), silver stress (182), EUR/USD volatile up (136), bitcoin stress (154) and ether stress (199). Another 5 have fewer than 20 episodes: US stocks stress (19), oil volatile up (17), natural gas volatile up (19), bitcoin stress (15) and ether stress (16). Silver stress looks dramatic (+4.4% against +0.7% for all days, 66.1% positive) but rests on 182 days in 21 episodes, and oil's volatile-up regime (+5.5%, 71.8% positive) on 286 days in 17 episodes.

Sign changes also argue for caution. The gap flips between the first and second half of the sample for oil (-0.27 then +1.64 points) and broad commodities (-0.15 then +2.11). Bitcoin's volatile-up regime averaged +9.8% over 469 days while ether's averaged -1.8% over 249, two similar assets with opposite results. Gold's pooled gap (-0.15 points on the mean, +0.04 on the median) is simply flat.

Takeaways

  • The only repeated finding is wider outcomes after high volatility: 9 of 10 markets. Direction is mixed: 5 better, 5 worse.
  • Natural gas is the clearest negative (-3.90 points, 802 days, 61 episodes), US stocks and broad commodities the clearest positives, but none of the ten differences is two standard errors from zero.
  • US stock stress was followed by +3.5% on average and 78.0% positive months, from just 19 episodes.
  • Cells with under 200 days or under 20 episodes, such as ether stress, say very little.
  • These are descriptions of history over 8 to 16 years, not forecasts.
