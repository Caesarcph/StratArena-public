Do Trend Followers Exit Before Stock Crashes? 12 Events Tested

Across 12 US stock sell-offs in the site's event replays, trend strategies were already out when the fall began only 29.9% of the time on average, less often than on a normal day (38.6%). They still lost just 4.7% against the market's 15.0%, because they left during the fall.

Trend FollowingMean ReversionCrashEvent Study

The question

A common claim is that trend-following rules "get you out before the crash". The site's event replays let us check it on stock-market data. Each event fixes a window from a peak to a trough in US stocks (SPY), and the engine records for every strategy whether it was in the market going in, what it returned over the window and what it returned over the following 63 trading days.

The question here is narrower than the claim: in the 12 stock sell-offs on the site, how often were trend-following strategies already out when the fall began, compared with mean-reversion strategies, and what did each family lose and then earn back?

How it was tested

The sample is every curated event on the site whose primary market is US stocks and whose direction is down: 12 events, from the spring 2010 correction to the 2025 tariff sell-off. The market fell 15.0% on average in the windows (median 12.8%). Five events fell more than 15%, and seven fell between 5% and 15%. The deepest three were the 2020 crash (-33.7%), the 2022 bear market (-24.5%) and the late-2018 sell-off (-19.3%). Windows range from 6 to 195 trading days, and no two overlap. An ongoing, automatically detected episode is not included, and neither are events on other markets.

Families come from strategy tags, with each strategy placed in the first matching family in the order trend, momentum, mean reversion, volatility, other. That gives 24 trend strategies (288 strategy-event pairs) and 14 or 15 mean-reversion strategies per event (176 pairs). COMBO-03 is excluded because its code looks ahead. "Already out" is the engine's inference: the strategy's equity did not move on any of the last three sessions up to the window start. Results are walk-forward backtests on daily data, before trading costs.

All averages below are simple means across the 12 events, with each event counting once, of the family's mean result in that event.

How often each family was already out

Trend strategies were out at the start 29.9% of the time on average (median 25.0%); pooled, that is 86 of 288 pairs. In only 2 of the 12 events were most of them out: the early-2016 sell-off (67%) and the 2025 tariff sell-off (58%). Mean-reversion strategies were out 96.7% of the time (170 of 176 pairs), and in all 12 events most of them were out.

Neither number is foresight. Averaged over all days since each strategy's start, the engine would class trend strategies as out 38.6% of the time and mean-reversion strategies 82.6% of the time. So at the start of a sell-off trend strategies were out 8.8 points less often than normal, which fits a market that was rising until its peak, and mean-reversion strategies were out 14.1 points more often, which fits rules that buy only after declines. The claim that trend following gets you out before the fall is not supported at the moment the fall begins.

Losses during the events and what followed

In the event windows, trend strategies lost 4.7% on average (median across events 3.9%) and mean-reversion strategies lost 4.0% (median 2.8%), against the market's 15.0%. Both families beat the market on average in all 12 events, by 10.4 points for trend and 11.0 for mean reversion.

Within trend, the pairs that were in at the start lost 6.1% on average (202 pairs) and those that were out lost 1.2% (86 pairs). Being out at the start did help, but most were in and then left. For mean reversion, only 6 pairs were in at the start, too few to compare.

Over the following 63 trading days, trend strategies averaged +3.0% (median 2.6%, positive in 11 of 12 events), mean-reversion strategies +4.8% (median 4.4%, positive in all 12), and the market +15.9% (median 12.9%). Both families caught a small part of the rebound.

A few events dominate

The averages hide how concentrated the results are. The three deepest events (2020, 2022, late 2018) account for 39% of the summed window loss for trend strategies and 50% for mean reversion. The 2020 crash alone cost mean-reversion strategies 15.5% on average, and without it the family's average loss falls from 4.0% to 3.0%. Trend strategies lost most in the 2022 bear market (8.4%), then 2020 (7.8%) and the 2011 sell-off (7.3%); without 2020 their average is 4.4%, and without 2020 and 2022 it is 4.0%.

The rebound is even more concentrated. For trend strategies, the 2025 tariff sell-off (+8.3%) and the 2020 crash (+7.6%) make up 44% of the summed 63-day returns, and the average of the other ten events is only 2.0%. For mean reversion, the top two events (2020, +9.8%, and the 2011 sell-off, +6.7%) make up 29%, and the other ten average 4.1%.

Split by depth, the five events worse than -15% show the same families losing 6.5% (trend) and 6.0% (mean reversion), with 26.7% and 97.3% out at the start; the seven shallower events show 3.3% and 2.7%, with 32.1% and 96.2% out. With five and seven events, these splits describe, they do not test.

The surprising part

The two families differ sharply in positioning, yet ended up with similar losses. Mean-reversion strategies were almost always flat at the start, yet lost 4.0% on average, close to the 4.7% of trend strategies that were mostly invested. Mean reversion lost only 1.2% in 2022, against 8.4% for trend, and lost most in 2020, 15.5% against 7.8%.

What protected trend strategies was not being out early but moving out as prices fell. That cost them part of the rebound (+3.0% against +15.9%). For mean reversion the figures suggest, without proving it, that protection depends on the rule having no buy signal when the fall starts, and in 2020 the family lost 15.5%.

Caveats

Twelve events is a small sample, and the largest few drive every average. The events were chosen by the site for their size, so the sample leaves out the many dips that never became sell-offs. The "already out" test is inferred from equity curves, and a rarely trading mean-reversion rule will often look out. The windows start at the peak, which is known in hindsight: a strategy cannot know it is at the start of a fall. Family means treat each strategy equally, so they say little about any one rule. Results are before trading costs and for the site's walk-forward setup only.

Takeaways

  • Trend strategies were out at the start of the fall in 29.9% of cases (12 events), below their 38.6% all-days norm, so "out before the crash" is not what the replays show.
  • They lost 4.7% against the market's 15.0% by exiting during the fall, and rebounded +3.0% against the market's +15.9% over the next 63 days.
  • Mean-reversion strategies were out 96.7% of the time yet lost 4.0%, with 2020 alone costing them 15.5%.
  • The 2020 crash, 2022 bear market and late 2018 account for 39% to 50% of summed losses; the two largest rebounds make up 44% of the trend family's rebound total.
  • Twelve events cannot separate skill from circumstance. Read these figures as a description of what happened, not a forecast.
