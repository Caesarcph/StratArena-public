Do Moving-Average Strategies Beat Buy-and-Hold on Bitcoin?

Over the last five years six moving-average systems all had a shallower maximum drawdown than holding bitcoin (−36.7% to −51.2% against −75.9%), and four beat its 2.6% annual return. Over the full history since September 2014 only one of six beat holding on return (MACD, 59.0% against 52.0%).

Six rules built on moving averages

The moving-average family on the site is larger than its name suggests. Six long-only systems are pure moving-average rules, all tested on daily bitcoin and ether data, before trading costs:

  • Dual Moving Average (TREND-05): long while the 50-day simple average is above the 200-day.
  • Triple Moving Average (TREND-13): long when the 50, 100 and 200-day averages are stacked in order, flat when they stack the other way, and it holds its previous position when they are mixed.
  • Kaufman Adaptive Moving Average (TREND-11): long when price is above an adaptive average that speeds up in clean trends and slows in chop, and the average is rising.
  • HMA Crossover (HMA-01): long while a 9-day Hull average is above a 21-day Hull average.
  • MACD Strategy (TREND-12): long while the 12/26-day exponential-average difference is above its own 9-day average.
  • Trend + Macro Filter (EVENT-01): the 50/200 rule of TREND-05, but forced flat on the first three trading days of every month. The code has no economic calendar; that is what "macro filter" means here.

Three more use a moving average inside a larger rule: MACD + RSI Filter (COMBO-02), Regime Switch (REG-01, price above its 200-day average and 60-day volatility under 25%) and Trend + Mean Reversion Hybrid (COMBO-04, buys RSI dips above the 200-day average). Walk-forward selection retunes the settings of five of the six pure rules (3 to 6 distinct settings across the bitcoin folds); TREND-05 and REG-01 have no tunable parameters and always run as described. Bitcoin data starts on 2014-09-17 and the first walk-forward test period on 2017-09-17; ether starts on 2017-11-09, first test period 2020-11-09.

The last five years: a bear market at the start

The five-year window starts on 2021-11-11, three days after bitcoin's November 2021 high. Buy-and-hold made 2.6% a year with a Sharpe ratio of 0.25 and a maximum drawdown of −75.9%.

Four of the six systems beat that return: MACD (10.6%), Trend + Macro Filter (9.8%), HMA Crossover (5.8%) and Dual Moving Average (3.2%). Triple MA (0.6%) and the Kaufman system (−0.6%) did not. All six had a shallower maximum drawdown, from −36.7% (Trend + Macro Filter) to −51.2% (HMA Crossover), while being in the market 40% to 53% of days. Only three of six had a better Sharpe ratio than holding.

Two things stand out. The systems cut the drawdown by 24.7 to 39.2 percentage points, yet a drawdown of −36.7% to −51.2% is still a deep hole. And the filtered relatives did better still on risk: Trend + Mean Reversion Hybrid (COMBO-04) had a −26.8% drawdown and 4.7% a year, but was in the market only 11.5% of days.

The full history reverses the picture

Since 2014-09-17 (12.2 years) buy-and-hold bitcoin compounded at 52.0% a year with a −83.4% maximum drawdown and a Sharpe ratio of 0.80. Only MACD beat it on return, at 59.0%. The others ran from 44.5% (Trend + Macro Filter) to 49.5% (Dual Moving Average), with 43% to 58% of days in the market.

All six beat holding on Sharpe ratio (0.82 to 1.05) and all six had a shallower drawdown, but the shallowest was still −56.1% (Kaufman) and the deepest −73.7% (Trend + Macro Filter). The three filtered relatives gave up most of the return: 15.8% for MACD + RSI Filter, 4.9% for the Hybrid and 3.8% for Regime Switch, which was in the market 3.4% of days.

The fast systems trade a great deal. Over the full history, the HMA Crossover changed position 652 times (53.7 a year), the Kaufman system 470 and MACD 314, against 21 for the dual and triple averages (1.7 a year). Each change costs money that these results leave out.

Year by year

Holding bitcoin rose in eight of the eleven full calendar years from 2015 to 2025 and fell in 2018, 2022 and 2025. Across the six systems and those eleven years:

  • In the eight up years a system beat holding in 8 of 48 system-years. In 2017 holding gained 1,368.9% and the median system 1,142.5%; in 2020 it was 303.2% against 121.0% and none of the six beat holding; in 2023, 155.4% against 45.8%.
  • In the three down years a system beat holding in 14 of 18 system-years, but none of the 18 made money. In 2018 holding lost 73.6% and the median system 30.0%; in 2022 holding lost 64.3% and the median system 28.9%; in 2025 holding lost 6.3% and the median system 8.6%, with only two of six doing better than holding.

In 2026 through 2026-11-11, holding is down 15.5% and the median system down 9.0%, with five of six ahead of holding. The trade is the same every time: less in the up years, less lost in the down years. Whether it pays depends on how many of each the sample contains.

The 2022 crypto bear market

Measured peak to trough (2021-11-08 to 2022-11-21, 378 days), bitcoin fell 76.6%. None of the nine systems made money in that window. Losses ran from −27.2% (Trend + Macro Filter) to −52.3% (HMA Crossover), median −46.3% for the six pure rules, with 18% to 53% of days in the market.

The calendar year looks much kinder: holding lost 64.3% in 2022 and the six systems lost between 6.9% (Dual Moving Average) and 42.8% (Kaufman). The gap is timing. From the peak to the end of 2021 bitcoin fell 31.5%, and the dual and triple averages fell the same 31.5%, so they had not stepped aside yet. They then lost only 6.9% and 18.1% from 2022-01-01 to the trough. The faster systems, MACD and HMA, lost 15.8% and 22.2% before year end and a further 39.1% and 38.7% afterwards, and were in the market 52% and 53% of days in the bear window.

Ether: the same pattern, a harsher sample

Ether data covers 9.0 years from 2017-11-09. Holding made 22.9% a year over the full period with a −94.0% maximum drawdown, and −15.4% a year over five years (−79.0% drawdown). Four of six systems beat holding over the full period (HMA Crossover 43.7%, Kaufman 32.2%, MACD 31.7%, Triple MA 23.1%) and all six had a shallower drawdown, from −59.7% to −78.9%.

Over five years five of six beat holding, but only two had a positive return: MACD at 3.9% and HMA Crossover at 1.6%. In the 2022 bear (2021-11-08 to 2022-06-18, −79.4%) the six lost between 22.5% (Kaufman) and 47.6% (HMA Crossover). Across the four down years from 2018 to 2025, systems beat holding in 18 of 24 system-years, yet made money in only 8; MACD gained 14.0% in calendar 2022.

What the numbers cannot tell you

Bitcoin has only 12.2 years of data, so every conclusion rests on a handful of episodes: the 2017 and 2020 rallies and the 2018 and 2022 crashes. The 2014 and 2026 figures are partial years, and the ether systems were in the market on average only 13% of days in 2017 (from 9 November) and 22% in 2018. Results are before trading costs and the faster systems trade often. Daily returns of these systems correlate 0.61 to 0.81 with holding, so they behave more like a partial holding than like an independent bet.

Takeaways

  • Over five years the six systems cut maximum drawdown by 24.7 to 39.2 percentage points against holding and four beat its return; over the full history only MACD beat the return.
  • The systems lag in the up years and lose less in the down years; whether they win over a window depends on how many bull years it holds.
  • The slow 50/200 averages matched holding through the first leg of the 2022 fall and only protected the rest; MACD and HMA were in the market about half the days of the bear window and lost more.
  • Filtered variants (Hybrid, Regime Switch) traded return for very small exposure and should be read as different products.
  • Ether follows the same pattern with deeper holes.
