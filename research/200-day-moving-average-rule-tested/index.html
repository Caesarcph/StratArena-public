Does the 200-Day Moving Average Rule Work? Ten Markets Tested

Holding only while price is above its 200-day average had a smaller maximum drawdown than buy-and-hold in all ten markets and a higher Sharpe ratio in nine, but it trailed on return in US stocks, gold and bitcoin and switched 1,140 times in total, before costs.

200-Day Moving AverageTrendDrawdownWhipsaw

The rule

The saying is that you should own a market only while its price is above its 200-day moving average and step aside when it is below. The claim checker turns this into a mechanical rule: when a day closes above the 200-day simple average, hold the market from the next day; when a day closes below it, hold cash from the next day. Cash earns nothing, there is no stop-loss, and trading costs are not included.

The only variant in the claims data is the same rule with a 1% band, which needs a close more than 1% above the average to buy and more than 1% below to sell. A 10-month average variant is not in the claims data and was not tested. The site's Faber TAA (MOM-05) applies a 200-day test once a month across a four-market portfolio; it is not part of this comparison.

How it was tested

The rule was run on US stocks, gold, silver, oil, natural gas, broad commodities, agriculture, EUR/USD, bitcoin and ether, using daily closes up to 2026-11-11. A position decided at the close of day t earns the return of day t+1, and the average on day t uses closes up to day t only. After the 200-day warm-up the tested periods start on 2010-10-18 for most markets, 2010-10-07 for EUR/USD, 2015-04-04 for bitcoin and 2018-05-27 for ether.

The checker counts the rule as working only if it cuts the maximum drawdown by at least a quarter and improves the Sharpe ratio, in the full period and in both halves of it. The switch counts, whipsaw years and worst-stretch figures below were computed for this article from the same data and the same convention.

Return, drawdown and time in the market

Against buy-and-hold over the same dates, the rule had a smaller maximum drawdown in all 10 markets, at least a quarter smaller in 7, a higher Sharpe ratio in 9 and a higher annual return in 7. It was in the market between 25% of days (natural gas) and 85% (US stocks).

  • US stocks: 10.6% a year against 14.3%, Sharpe 0.92 against 0.87, maximum drawdown -19.8% against -33.7%, in the market 85% of days.
  • Gold: 5.4% against 6.4% a year, drawdown -33.5% against -45.6%, in the market 61% of days.
  • Bitcoin (from 2015-04-04): 59.0% against 63.1% a year, drawdown -69.9% against -83.4%, in the market 61% of days.
  • Ether (from 2018-05-27): 40.0% against 16.3% a year, drawdown -70.9% against -86.4%.
  • Agriculture: 3.0% against 0.4% a year, drawdown -31.3% against -61.9%, in the market 47% of days.
  • Natural gas: -7.2% against -23.2% a year, in the market 25% of days.

The three return shortfalls are US stocks, gold and bitcoin. The rule's largest gains over buy-and-hold are in markets where holding did badly or lost money, such as natural gas and agriculture.

The worst stretch for buy-and-hold

For each market, take the peak-to-trough fall that gave buy-and-hold its maximum drawdown and ask what the rule did over the same dates. In US stocks that was 2020-02-19 to 2020-03-23: buy-and-hold lost 33.7%, the rule lost 17.4%. It first went flat on 2020-02-28 and was long on only 8 of the 23 trading days.

EUR/USD, from 2011-04-29 to 2022-09-28, fell 35.4% for buy-and-hold against 2.3% for the rule. Broad commodities, from 2011-04-08 to 2020-04-27, fell 66.1% against 25.9%. The rule lost less than buy-and-hold in that window in all 10 markets. Bitcoin in calendar 2022 is the extreme case: the rule was in cash all year with no switches, returning 0.0% against -64.3% for buy-and-hold.

The price of switching

Across the ten markets the rule changed position 1,140 times, from 46 for ether to 185 for oil. In 107 of 149 market-years (years with at least 200 days of data) it switched four or more times, and in 63 of those it earned less than buy-and-hold. These are what traders call whipsaws: a close just above the average, a close just below, and a loss each way.

US stocks show it year by year. In 2011 the rule switched 12 times and returned -9.1% against +1.9%; in 2015 it switched 8 times and returned -4.4% against +1.2%. In four years (2013, 2017, 2021 and 2024) it never switched and matched buy-and-hold exactly. In 2019 and 2023 it was in and out and earned 16.6% and 15.4% against 31.2% and 26.2%. None of this includes spread, commission or slippage, which would be paid on every one of those 1,140 switches.

Does a band fix it?

The 1% band halves the trading: 556 switches in total instead of 1,140. But the benefit shrinks too. The band version beat buy-and-hold on annual return in 6 markets instead of 7, on Sharpe in 6 instead of 9, and cut the drawdown by a quarter in 6 instead of 7. US stocks fell to 9.5% a year with Sharpe 0.83, below buy-and-hold's 0.87.

By the checker's rules the plain rule is "partly" holding, with 3 markets clearly passing (natural gas, agriculture and EUR/USD), and the band version is "does not hold", with 1. A band avoids some false signals but also enters and exits later, and on these ten histories that trade-off did not pay.

What the numbers cannot tell you

The three clear passes are all markets where buy-and-hold was poor (natural gas -23.2% a year, EUR/USD -1.4%, agriculture 0.4%). In US stocks the rule's edge is concentrated in a few episodes: in the first half of the sample (2010-10-18 to 2018-10-26) its drawdown was -17.0% against -18.6% and its Sharpe 0.91 against 0.92, essentially no difference; its drawdown advantage appears in the second half (-19.8% against -33.7%), which contains the 2020 crash.

Each market is one price path, with 38 entries for bitcoin and 23 for ether. Results depend on a few crash episodes, the periods start in 2010 or later, and the ten markets are not independent of one another. Everything is before costs.

Takeaways

  • The 200-day rule's reliable effect in this test was smaller drawdowns (all 10 markets), not higher returns (7 of 10, with US stocks, gold and bitcoin behind).
  • Its price was 1,140 switches and 63 whipsaw market-years out of 149; costs are not included.
  • A 1% band halved the trading but lost much of the benefit, so reducing whipsaws is not free.
