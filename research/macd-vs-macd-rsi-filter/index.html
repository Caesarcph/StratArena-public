Does an RSI Filter Improve MACD? A Ten-Market Backtest

Adding an RSI filter to a MACD crossover (COMBO-02 against TREND-12) cut trades on all ten markets and the five-year maximum drawdown on nine, but raised the Sharpe ratio on only six. The filter kept the strategy invested on 33% of days instead of 50%, and it cost the most return on bitcoin.

MACDRSIFilterTrend

Two rules, read from the code

MACD Strategy (TREND-12) is long whenever the MACD line (by default the 12-day exponential average minus the 26-day one) is above its 9-day signal line, and flat otherwise. It checks that state every day and always holds 100% or nothing.

MACD + RSI Filter (COMBO-02) uses the same lines but adds three RSI rules. It enters when MACD crosses above the signal line and RSI is below 70, or when MACD is already above the signal line and RSI is below 30. It exits on a cross down, when RSI rises above 80, or when MACD is below the signal line and RSI is above 70. After an RSI exit while MACD is still bullish, the way back in is the next cross up (or RSI falling below 30 while MACD is bullish). This RSI uses exponential smoothing with a span of 14 days, which is not the Wilder smoothing many charting tools use.

The strategy page also says sell signals are ignored when RSI is oversold. The code has no such rule: a cross down always exits. In the walk-forward test TREND-12 chose its fast and slow lengths from 10, 12 and 14 and 21, 26 and 31 days; COMBO-02 chose the fast length (10, 12 or 14) and the RSI length (11, 14 or 17), with the 70, 30 and 80 thresholds fixed. Results are before trading costs, and a position decided at the close of day t earns the return of day t+1.

What the filter changes

Over the five years to 11 November 2026, TREND-12 was invested on 50% of days on average across the ten markets and COMBO-02 on 33%. TREND-12 made 1,179 trades in total and COMBO-02 971, 18% fewer (the site counts each entry and each exit as a trade); on every market the filter traded less, by between 10% and 29%.

The days it removed are the ones where MACD was long and the filter was flat, and their value depends on the market. On US stocks that was 15% of days, and the asset fell 5.8% on them. On natural gas it was 12% of days, with a 67.2% fall. On silver it was 26% of days, with a 37.7% gain; on bitcoin 18% of days and a 66.0% gain; on ether 16% of days and a 39.9% gain. On the strongest trends, the filter sat out days that carried much of the gain.

Five-year results on ten markets

Over the five years the filter had the higher Sharpe ratio on six markets and the smaller maximum drawdown on nine.

  • Sharpe improved: US stocks (0.32 to 0.51), gold (0.40 to 0.49), natural gas (-0.49 to -0.13), broad commodities (0.44 to 0.62), agriculture (0.24 to 0.59) and EUR/USD (-0.18 to 0.06).
  • Sharpe fell: silver (0.55 to 0.37), oil (-0.12 to -0.23), bitcoin (0.39 to 0.11) and ether (0.26 to 0.13).
  • Drawdown: silver -46.7% to -28.1%, broad commodities -30.7% to -17.0%, gold -23.6% to -13.1%, US stocks -17.1% to -12.3%. The exception was EUR/USD, -10.1% to -11.3%.
  • Annual return: higher with the filter on six markets. The largest losses were bitcoin (10.6% to 0.1% a year), silver (11.3% to 4.4%) and ether (3.9% to -2.0%); the largest gain was natural gas (-29.0% to -11.4%).

Neither version was a good substitute for holding. TREND-12 beat holding's Sharpe on 2 of 10 markets and COMBO-02 on 3. On US stocks they made 2.8% and 4.4% a year against 11.9% for holding (Sharpe 0.74).

The full period

Over the full history (since January 2010 for US stocks, September 2014 for bitcoin and November 2017 for ether) the picture is similar but not identical. The filter again raised the Sharpe ratio on six markets (US stocks, oil, natural gas, broad commodities, agriculture and EUR/USD) and lowered it on gold, silver, bitcoin and ether. It reduced the maximum drawdown on eight, not on oil or agriculture. Total trades fell from 3,448 to 2,804.

Bitcoin shows the price of the filter. Over the full history TREND-12 made 59.0% a year with a Sharpe of 1.05 and a -57.9% drawdown, against 52.0%, 0.80 and -83.4% for holding. COMBO-02 made 15.8% with a Sharpe of 0.52 and a -45.9% drawdown. On US stocks the filter was better: 6.2% a year, Sharpe 0.72 and a -14.9% drawdown, against 5.8%, 0.63 and -17.1% for MACD alone.

The years it helped and hurt

We compared each strategy's calendar-year return on each market for the 16 full years from 2010 to 2025 (147 market-years). The filter did better in 71 of them and worse in 76; the median difference was -0.4 percentage points. It was ahead on a majority of markets in only 6 of the 16 years.

The year it helped most was 2022. The filter beat plain MACD on 8 of 10 markets, with a median gain of 4.0 points: the median market lost 8.3% under MACD and gained 1.6% with the filter. US stocks went from -7.7% to +1.1%, gold from -6.4% to +2.2%, silver from -9.0% to +5.5% and natural gas from -17.6% to +5.8%. The next-best years were 2015 (better on 7 of 9 markets) and 2010.

The years it hurt most were 2021 (better on only 4 of 10 markets; median -5.4 points) and 2020 (3 of 10; median -3.8). The largest single losses were in crypto: bitcoin returned 916.0% in 2017 under MACD and 78.1% with the filter, and in 2020 192.8% against 86.2%. The years 2010 to 2012 come before the first walk-forward fold for the ETFs and use default parameters.

A drawdown, and what the numbers cannot tell you

MACD's worst five-year drawdown on silver was -46.7%, from 28 January to 16 July 2026. Over the same dates the filter lost 23.6%, and silver itself fell 52.3%. In that stretch the filter worked as intended. It cost silver 6.9 points of annual return over the five years.

The evidence is thin. On US stocks the probabilistic Sharpe ratio was 0.76 for TREND-12 and 0.87 for COMBO-02; the deflated Sharpe ratio, which allows for the number of strategies compared, was 0.22 and 0.37. A difference of 0.1 to 0.2 in the Sharpe ratio over five years should not be read as skill. Both systems were also re-fitted every six months, so the comparison is between two adaptive procedures, not two fixed rules. At the site's cost assumption of 10 basis points per unit of turnover, the average drag over the ten markets was 2.2% a year for TREND-12 and 1.8% for COMBO-02, so costs would widen the gap a little in the filter's favour.

Takeaways

  • The RSI filter is mostly an exit and entry rule that keeps MACD out of fast, overbought moves: 33% of days invested instead of 50%, and 18% fewer trades.
  • It cut the five-year maximum drawdown on 9 of 10 markets and raised the Sharpe ratio on 6, so it is a risk reducer more than a return booster.
  • It helped most in 2022 and hurt most in crypto-led years such as 2017 and 2020; across 147 market-years it was close to a coin flip.
  • Bitcoin paid the most for the filter (10.6% a year over five years against 0.1%), and silver and ether also gave up return.
