Golden Cross and Death Cross Tested on Ten Markets

A long-only 50/200 moving-average rule beat buy-and-hold on return in 7 of 10 markets and cut the maximum drawdown in 9, but it did not help US stocks, gold or bitcoin, and no market showed a statistically clear edge after either cross (107 golden and 106 death crosses in total, before costs).

What the rule says

A golden cross is the day the 50-day simple average of closing prices rises above the 200-day average. A death cross is the day it falls below. The popular reading is that a golden cross is bullish and a death cross is bearish.

To make that testable, the rule is: hold the market from the day after the 50-day average closes above the 200-day average, and hold cash from the day after it closes below. There is no stop-loss, no position sizing, cash earns nothing and trading costs are not included. The site's Dual Moving Average (TREND-05) uses the same 50/200 state in its code, although its description mentions volatility filters and ATR sizing that the code does not contain.

How it was tested

The claim checker runs the rule on ten markets: US stocks, gold, silver, oil, natural gas, broad commodities, agriculture, EUR/USD, bitcoin and ether. A position decided at the close of day t earns the return of day t+1, and the averages on day t use closes up to day t only.

The first 200 days warm up the average, so the tested period is 2010-10-18 to 2026-11-11 for most markets, 2010-10-07 for EUR/USD, 2015-04-04 for bitcoin and 2018-05-27 for ether. Three questions were asked: how the long-only rule compares with buy-and-hold, whether days spent in the golden state earned more than days outside it, and what the 63 trading days after each cross looked like.

Return and drawdown against buy-and-hold

Over those periods the rule had a higher annual return than buy-and-hold in 7 of 10 markets and a higher Sharpe ratio in 6. The maximum drawdown was smaller in 9 of 10 markets and at least a quarter smaller in 5. All figures are before trading costs.

  • Natural gas (2010-10-18 to 2026-11-11): buy-and-hold lost 23.2% a year with a -98.9% maximum drawdown; the rule lost 2.1% a year with a -49.7% drawdown, in the market only 25% of days, over 9 entries.
  • Oil: -1.4% a year against -4.4%, with a drawdown of -70.8% against -95.3%.
  • Ether (2018-05-27 to 2026-11-11): 23.5% a year against 16.3%, drawdown -78.0% against -86.4%, over 9 entries.
  • US stocks: 9.7% a year against 14.3%, Sharpe 0.72 against 0.87, and the same -33.7% drawdown. It was in the market 84% of days with 8 entries.
  • Gold: 5.0% against 6.4% a year, Sharpe 0.43 against 0.45, drawdown -35.1% against -45.6%.
  • Bitcoin (2015-04-04 to 2026-11-11): 52.3% against 63.1% a year, Sharpe 0.87 against 0.90, drawdown -69.2% against -83.4%.

The pattern is that the rule helped most where buy-and-hold was poor (natural gas, oil, agriculture at 1.9% against 0.4%). In US stocks and bitcoin, where holding was rewarded, it trailed.

What followed the crosses

Across the ten markets there were 107 golden crosses and 106 death crosses, from 7 to 17 per market and 7 of each for US stocks. For the forward-return test, events had to be at least 63 trading days apart, which left 104 golden and 103 death events (period 2010-01-01 to 2026-11-11; bitcoin from 2014-09-17, ether from 2017-11-09).

After a golden cross the average 63-day return was above the market's average 63-day return on all days in 6 of 10 markets and below it in 4. It was negative in 3: broad commodities (-3.3%, 11 events), agriculture (-1.5%, 9 events) and EUR/USD (-0.1%, 12 events). The best-looking cases were natural gas (+12.9% against -4.4% on all days, 9 events) and bitcoin (+23.0% against +13.5%, 11 events).

After a death cross, the average 63-day return was above the all-days average in 4 markets and below it in 6, but 6 of 10 markets still averaged a positive 63-day return after the bearish signal. No market showed a statistically clear difference after either cross: none had the 20 events the checker requires, and the largest sample was 17 (oil, golden cross).

Where it clearly did not help

US stocks give the plainest case. The 2020 fall from 2020-02-19 to 2020-03-23 was -33.7%, and the rule was long on all 23 trading days of that window, so its maximum drawdown was identical to buy-and-hold. The death cross arrived on 2020-03-31, after the low, and the next 63 trading days returned +20.2%.

That was not unusual. Of the 7 death crosses in US stocks (2011-08-17 to 2025-04-16), 6 were followed by gains over the next 63 trading days, averaging +8.3% across all seven; only the one on 2022-03-16 was followed by a fall (-12.7%). Calendar 2022 was where the rule did help: -8.3% against -18.2% for buy-and-hold.

The second problem is short-lived signals. Of the 107 golden states, 28 lasted fewer than 63 trading days (a state still running at the end of the data is counted by its length so far). Each of those is a round trip that in practice would cost spread and commission, which this test leaves out.

What the numbers cannot tell you

The claim checker's verdict is "partly". In 8 of 10 markets, days in the golden state earned more than days outside it, with a median gap of +7.9% a year, but in none was the gap statistically clear (|t| of at least 2). Over all ten markets the rule made 112 entries, between 8 and 17 per market, so every market result rests on a handful of regime changes.

The ten markets are not independent, they share one 2010-2026 history with a long US bull market and a long commodity slump, and bitcoin and ether cover only the periods listed above. The site's own Dual Moving Average (TREND-05) lands in the same place on its full stored history: its Sharpe ratio is above buy-and-hold in 6 of 10 markets, the same count as this test.

Takeaways

  • As a long-only filter, 50/200 mostly reduced drawdown in markets that fell (natural gas, oil, agriculture), not in US stocks, where the -33.7% 2020 drawdown was untouched.
  • Neither cross was a reliable forecast: after a golden cross, 4 of 10 markets did worse than their average, and 6 of 10 still gained on average after a death cross.
  • With 7 to 17 crosses per market, no result here is statistically clear, and trading costs are not included.
