Donchian 55/20 vs Turtle 20/10: A Ten-Market Backtest

Before trading costs, the slow Donchian Breakout (TREND-02, 55-day high in, 20-day low out) made 774 entries across ten markets against 1,979 for Turtle Trading (TURTLE-01, 20/10), and had the higher full-period Sharpe ratio on seven of the ten markets (mean 0.38 vs 0.34). Over the last five years the gap was wider: 0.41 vs 0.21.

DonchianTurtle TradingBreakoutTrend

The two rules, from the code

Donchian Breakout (TREND-02) goes long when the close is above the highest close of the previous 55 days, and goes to cash when the close is below the lowest close of the previous 20 days. Between the two signals it holds whatever it held before. Turtle Trading (TURTLE-01) is the same logic with entry and exit windows of 20 and 10 days.

Three things worth knowing from the code. Both are long only. Both use closing prices only, not intraday highs and lows. And neither has the ATR position sizing or stops mentioned in the strategy descriptions: the code is simply 100% invested or 0%. Donchian has no adjustable settings, so it runs the same 55/20 throughout. Turtle's windows are re-chosen every six months from a grid around 20 and 10 (entry 16 to 24, exit 8 to 12), after an initial three years on 20/10.

Results are before trading costs, for the last five years to 2026-11-11 and for the full period (2010-01-04 for most markets, 2014-09-17 for bitcoin, 2017-11-09 for ether).

Best, worst and the head-to-head

Donchian's best market was broad commodities over five years (Sharpe 0.79) and bitcoin over the full period (0.98). Its worst was natural gas over five years and EUR/USD over the full period. Turtle's best were US stocks over five years (0.63 Sharpe, only a 13.3% drawdown) and bitcoin over the full period (1.05); its worst was natural gas on both windows (−13.6% a year and a 91.6% drawdown over the full period).

Donchian had the higher five-year Sharpe on eight of ten markets (mean 0.41 vs 0.21) and the shallower drawdown on eight (mean −28.5% vs −37.3%). Over the full period those counts were seven and seven, and the means 0.38 vs 0.34 and −38.8% vs −43.7%. Turtle's wins were on US stocks, bitcoin and ether over the full period. Eight markets had a positive annual return for Donchian on both windows; for Turtle it was seven over five years and eight over the full period.

Time in the market

The slow rule spends much less time invested. Donchian was in the market on a median 33% of days (range 20% to 54%) against Turtle's 38% (32% to 55%). Dividing days in the market by entries gives roughly 17.7 days per holding for Donchian and 8.3 for Turtle on average. On a given day they agree on being in or out a median 77% of the time.

The two systems also differ in how they behave once a trend ends. A 20-day exit stays in a falling market for a long time, while a 10-day exit gets out sooner, at the price of more whipsaw.

The worst calendar year in each market

In each market's worst full calendar year, both beat the market in every case, and the market fell an average of 42.8% in those years. Donchian averaged −10.2% and lost money in nine of ten; Turtle averaged −3.3% and lost money in seven of ten.

US stocks in 2022 shows the exit speed. The market fell 18.2%. Donchian was invested on only 14% of days that year and still lost 12.3%; Turtle, invested on 38% of days, lost 5.6%. Gold in 2013 (−28.3%) cost Donchian 5.7% and Turtle 4.5%, with Donchian in the market on 5% of days. Oil in 2020 (−67.8%) was good for both: Donchian gained 7.8% and Turtle 34.0%. Natural gas in 2023 (−64.0%) was not: −25.6% and −49.4%. On ether in 2018 (−82.4%), Turtle gained 39.3% while Donchian lost 21.0%; that year falls in the default-settings period of Turtle's walk-forward run.

Where breakouts fail

Breakouts need price to make a new high before they act. In a market that drifts, they buy near the top of a range and sell near the bottom of the next. EUR/USD shows it: neither system had a positive annual return over the full period (−0.9% and −1.3%), and natural gas lost money on both windows for both.

The numbers also leave things out. Turtle made 1,979 entries and Donchian 774, and no trading costs are included. A long-only version also says nothing about the short side that the original Turtle system traded. Bitcoin and ether results come from short histories, so they rest on few market cycles.

Takeaways

  • The slower 55/20 rule held up better than 20/10 on risk-adjusted terms on most markets, and traded about 39% as often (774 vs 1,979 entries).
  • Turtle's faster exit limited the damage in US stocks in 2022 and in oil in 2020, but did worse in natural gas in 2023 and EUR/USD in 2014.
  • Both are far less than fully invested (medians 33% and 38% of days), so they trail a rising market in return terms.
  • Neither adds position sizing or stops: the backtest is a plain in-or-out system, before trading costs.
