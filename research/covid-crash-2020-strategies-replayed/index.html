How Did 61 Strategies Handle the 2020 COVID Crash on US Stocks?

In the site's replay of 19 February to 23 March 2020, US stocks (SPY) fell 33.7%. Only 3 of 61 strategies made money, though 58 did better than buy-and-hold, but 38 were flat at the low and just 3 recaptured half of the 39.9% rebound that followed.

The question and the window

The site's event library defines the COVID-19 crash as a decline in SPY from 19 February to 23 March 2020. The data confirm the dates exactly: SPY fell 33.7% in 23 trading days, and its worst single day, 16 March, was -10.9%, the 18th trading day after the peak. The first close 10% below the peak came on 27 February, only 6 trading days in.

This article replays every strategy with SPY history over that window and asks four things: how many were already out before the worst of it, how deep each family's drawdown was, who was back in for the rebound, and what the same dates look like on gold and bitcoin. Results are walk-forward backtests on daily data, before trading costs. One strategy, COMBO-03, is left out because its code looks ahead, so the SPY sample is 61 strategies, not 62.

How the replay is measured

A strategy's return over the window is the change in its equity curve from the first to the last day. "In the market going in" is inferred, not recorded: the strategy's equity moved on at least one of the last three sessions up to the window start. "Rebound" means the 63 trading days after the low, the same window the site's event pages use. Families come from the strategy tags, and a strategy is counted in the first family that matches, in the order trend, momentum, mean reversion, volatility, other.

I added two checkpoints with the same in-the-market test: the first close 10% below the peak (27 February) and the close before the worst day (13 March). Everything below is one event on one market, so treat it as a case study, not a statistical result.

Who was already out

Going in, 37 of the 61 strategies were in the market and 24 were out or flat. By the 27 February checkpoint, 25 were out, and 16 of the 37 that started in had already left. By 13 March, the close before the worst day, 30 were out and 22 of the original 37 had left.

The outcome was lopsided. 58 of 61 did better than SPY's 33.7% fall, the median strategy lost 7.6%, and its median drawdown inside the window was 8.7%. 26 strategies lost less than 5% or gained. Three finished with a gain: Linear Regression Channel (TREND-14, +33.9%), Crisis Alpha (TAIL-01, +25.7%), which in the code is short-only in high volatility, and Connors RSI (MR-16, +9.4%). Three more ended exactly flat: ADX Trend Filter (ADX-01), Elder Triple Screen (TREND-19) and Hurst-Filtered Mean Reversion (MR-07). The last of these computes no Hurst exponent, only a z-score with a low-volatility filter. At the other end, 17 lost more than 20%, and the worst, CCI Mean Reversion (MR-05), lost 35.4%, slightly more than SPY itself.

How deep each family fell

Median window return and median drawdown inside the window, with the number of strategies in each family:

  • Trend (24 strategies): -4.7% and -6.1%; 4 were out going in; worst drawdown -33.7%.
  • Volatility (6): -6.7% and -6.7%; 2 out going in.
  • Other, including regime and tail-risk rules (12): -8.3% and -13.5%; 3 out going in.
  • Momentum (4): -12.1% and -14.5%; 1 out going in.
  • Mean reversion (15): -18.3% and -19.6%; 14 out going in; worst drawdown -35.4%.

Two things stand out. Trend strategies were mostly in on 19 February (20 of 24) yet lost the least: 15 of those 20 had left by 13 March. Mean reversion shows the opposite pattern. 14 of 15 were out at the start, yet this family lost the most, and 9 of 15 were holding at the low. Being out going in did not protect them: with 9 of 15 holding at the low, their entries evidently triggered during the fall.

Who re-entered in time

At the low, 23 strategies were in the market and 38 were flat. By 10 sessions later, 8 of the 38 had re-entered; by 21 sessions, 20 had, and 18 were still flat. Over the next 63 sessions, the median of those that re-entered by 21 sessions gained 9.8%, the median of those still flat gained 0.0%, and the median of those already holding at the low gained 12.2%. SPY gained 39.9%.

So nobody caught the rebound. 3 strategies captured at least half of it (Kalman Filter Mean Reversion MR-03, Monthly Seasonality SEAS-01 and Triple Moving Average TREND-13), and two of those, SEAS-01 and TREND-13, had lost 33.7% on the way down, the same as SPY, so they had effectively stayed fully invested. 24 captured between a quarter and a half, and 34 captured under a quarter, including 8 that made exactly nothing and 7 that lost money. Only 1 beat the market's rebound. The trend family had 1 of 24 above half and 15 under a quarter.

The two best crash performers paid for it: TREND-14 went from +33.9% to -16.4% in the 63 sessions after the low, and TAIL-01 from +25.7% to -27.9%. Measured from the peak to 63 sessions after the low, SPY was down 7.2%; 38 of 61 strategies beat that, with a median of -3.0%.

The same replay on gold and bitcoin

Same calendar dates, 55 strategies on each market (COMBO-03 again excluded).

Gold fell only 3.6% over the window, though its drawdown inside it reached 12.5%, and it rose 12.8% over the next 63 sessions. 34 strategies were in going in and 21 out. 13 made money, the median lost 3.6%, nobody lost more than 8.7%, and only 29 of 55 lost less than buy-and-hold. The trend family's median was -6.1%, worse than gold itself, while mean reversion's median was +1.1% with 13 of 14 out at the start. 23 of 34 flat-at-the-low strategies re-entered within 21 sessions; 18 of 55 captured half or more of gold's rebound, and 3 beat it.

Bitcoin fell 33.4% over the window with a drawdown inside it of 49.9%, then rose 38.8% over the next 63 sessions. 31 were in going in and 24 out; only 22 were in before SPY's worst day. 7 made money (TREND-14 +24.0%, Volatility Regime Strategy VOL-13 +23.4%, TAIL-01 +18.8% led), the median lost 5.8%, 16 lost more than 20% and the worst lost 40.8%. Mean reversion again fared worst (median -24.7%, 11 of 14 out going in). Re-entry mattered more here: the 11 of 30 flat-at-the-low strategies that re-entered within 21 sessions gained a median 22.1% afterwards, against 0.0% for those still flat. 14 of 55 captured half or more of the rebound and none beat it.

What the numbers cannot tell you

This is one event on one set of dates, set by the site rather than chosen by each strategy. A different start, a few days earlier or later, would change which strategies count as in the market. The in-the-market test is inferred from equity moves, and a strategy that holds a position that happens not to move would be misread. Family medians hide wide ranges inside families: the trend family contains both the best result and several that lost the full 33.7%. And the replay uses walk-forward results with no trading costs.

Takeaways

  • On SPY, 58 of 61 strategies did better than buy-and-hold in the window, but only 3 gained and median loss was 7.6%.
  • Family matters: trend lost a median 4.7%, mean reversion 18.3%, even though 14 of 15 mean-reversion strategies were out at the start.
  • Leaving early cost the rebound: 38 strategies were flat at the low, and only 3 recaptured half of the 39.9% bounce.
  • The best crash results were followed by the worst rebounds (TREND-14, TAIL-01).
  • On gold the same rules protected less than on stocks (29 of 55 beat buy-and-hold); on bitcoin the drawdown inside the window was 49.9%, deeper than the 33.4% end-to-end figure.
