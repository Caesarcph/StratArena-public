Changelog

What changed on Quant Arena, newest first.

Open the interactive version

Four new sections: market regimes, claim checker, event replays, famous strategies

2026-11-11

Market regimes shows each market's trend, volatility and strategy positioning every trading day. The claim checker tests 34 popular trading sayings on daily data and gives each a verdict. Event replays show how the strategies behaved in 35 major market events since 2010, plus recent sell-offs detected automatically. Famous strategies re-runs 22 well-known public portfolios and rule sets on our ten markets and says what had to be adapted.

Optional coffee support for continued research

2026-11-08

An optional Ko-fi link now appears in the footer, at the end of research articles and beside prepared or verified browser downloads. Contributions support data updates, backtest verification and new tools. All existing free downloads remain available. The support provider opens only when you choose the link; no payment widget loads automatically.

Compare portfolio additions and removals from pinned evidence

2026-11-07

A new comparison worksheet keeps before and after on identical shared dates, separates strategy and allocation fees, and shows gross/net returns, recovery, downside sample counts and signed exposure overlap. Frozen share links retain the original evidence; optional named saves stay in this browser. Independent sleeves do not net trades across strategies. Financing, borrowing, funding, taxes and market impact remain excluded.

Cost comparisons across 576 supported pairs

2026-11-07

All 576 supported strategy-market pairs now have separate 0, 5, 10 and 25 bp cost scenarios across seven windows. Strategies and matched benchmarks each pay charges on their own drift-adjusted one-way risky turnover after gross returns. Rankings compare identical asset universes, dates, input returns and window anchors; a basket labelled SPY is kept separate from single-market SPY. Fee totals use the disclosed initial-capital or start-close-capital units. Metrics use all original full-precision daily records, while curves are bounded display samples and metrics load by the selected universe. This independent fee-sensitivity view leaves existing before-cost Arena rankings intact. It models synthetic exposure charges, not actual broker fills or exact traded notional; financing, borrow, funding, taxes and market impact remain excluded.

Common-date AI season scheduled after public freeze

2026-11-07

OpenAI and Anthropic each retain six unchanged rules. Their prompt, original replies and receipt metadata were verified publicly on 7 November 2026 at 00:20 UTC, before scheduling. Season 1 runs from 9 November 2026 through 6 February 2027. It is scheduled, with no performance yet; rankings require at least 20 observations in every required market and complete matching windows. Late entrants belong to a later season.

Common-date AI seasons: two frozen provider submissions

2026-11-06

Two real CLI submissions, from OpenAI and Anthropic, now retain all six frozen rules each under one public prompt, with exact prompt/reply downloads, requested and reported model fields, freeze timestamps and operator-recorded receipts. The common-date season remains a draft: no start has been scheduled and no performance or rankings are published. Anthropic reports claude-sonnet-5 and discloses auxiliary claude-haiku-4-5-20251001 usage; the auxiliary purpose and underlying model revision are not established, and inference compute is not normalized across providers. The original single-assistant experiment remains separate with its original figures and disclosed execution-timing and rounded-input limitations. These CLI receipts are not provider-signed attestations.

Full-cohort evidence and causal calendar corrections

2026-11-06

Evidence now covers all 72 strategies and 576 supported pairs, including signed per-asset basket holdings. Daily records, configurations and archived source can be downloaded on demand; the standalone tool can fetch inputs and regenerate rules when the original input fingerprint matches. Every current run has original-input recomputation proof across 2,394,468 observations, including 11 newly corrected runs. Ten monthly basket rules now decide on the first observed new-month session using the preceding observation, with the engine lag retained. The turn-of-month rule uses a disclosed weekday approximation, not an exchange-holiday calendar. These corrections change historical results; old immutable runs and correction links remain available. Costs remain zero in this baseline; synthetic targets and rebalancing are not broker fills.

Auditable strategy evidence and direct engine positions

2026-11-06

Six representative strategies now have evidence bundles on SPY, gold and bitcoin: full-precision daily accounting, simulated position changes, actual selected parameters, source hashes and a standalone arithmetic replay script. The evidence index preserves prior corrected snapshots. Signals now come from engine targets rather than ratios of rounded returns, with target and effective exposure, per-market observation dates and independent data status. The current UTC date is excluded conservatively. Walk-forward boundary returns are recomputed from the previous published target, and skipped folds retain default targets; the evidence index quantifies these accounting changes. This is an 18-pair pilot, not full-site verification or broker fills.

Out-of-sample check on strategy pages

2026-11-04

Strategy pages now compare the Sharpe ratio that the walk-forward parameters had on their training windows with what they earned in the following six months, and with the default parameters over the same months. They also show how many settings in the parameter grid had a positive Sharpe ratio. Across the 486 strategy-and-market pairs with parameters to tune, the median Sharpe ratio fell from 0.32 on the training windows to 0.19 out of sample; the walk-forward glossary entry has the details.

EA pages show results month by month

2026-11-03

For each market with a reliable backtest, EA pages now show the share of months with a gain and the best and worst month, with dates. EA backtests are also re-run every month on the latest twelve months of hourly data.

Strategy results now always use walk-forward optimization

2026-11-03

Until now, each strategy kept its default parameters whenever they scored better over the whole history than the walk-forward run. That choice looked at the full sample, so it flattered the results: it applied to 106 of 576 strategy-and-market results, which scored a median of 5.6 points higher than their walk-forward versions. Every result now uses walk-forward optimization, so some numbers and rankings moved down.

Identical EA backtests are no longer counted

2026-11-03

85 EAs produced exactly the same backtest as another EA on every market — for example 14 unrelated EAs with identical results — which means the automatic conversion lost what made them different. Their 298 results are now labeled "Same as other EAs", with links to the twins, and are left out of the EA Ranking, the category statistics and the blow-up risk evidence. Reliable EA results go from 1,421 to 1,123. The article on 1,063 free MT5 EAs and the glossary have been updated.

2026-11-03

Under the backtest table, EA pages now show how each result ranks against other EAs of the same category on the same market, for example "EURUSD: better than 82% of 37". Only reliable results count, and only when at least ten EAs of the category have one.

Two new glossary terms: recovery factor and blow-up risk

2026-11-03

The glossary now has 20 terms. Recovery factor explains the number in the EA Ranking (the median EA backtest scored about zero). Blow-up risk explains broker stop-outs, how our backtests handle them and how the EA labels work: 61 of 307 gold backtests with default lot sizes ended in a stop-out, none on three currency pairs. EA pages link to both.

EA backtests rerun with realistic position sizes

2026-11-03

The converted EAs kept adding orders while one was still pending and treated lots as units, so positions grew far beyond the EA's lot size and most P&L figures were wrong. All 1,063 EAs were rerun on four markets with MT5 lot sizes, 1:100 leverage, one order at a time and a broker-style stop-out. Three conversion bugs that kept some EAs from ever trading were fixed. The EA Ranking now also shows the recovery factor and on how many markets each EA made money. Results that rely on a placeholder for a custom indicator are now labeled as approximations and left out of the ranking, and EA pages explain why an EA never traded when the cause is a conversion bug.
