AI Strategy Arena

Can an AI write a trading strategy that works? We asked an AI assistant for 6 trading rules, fixed every parameter, and now track them every trading day on nine markets against simply holding the market and against our 72 classic strategies.

Original single-assistant experiment

This legacy experiment retains its original figures. Its implementation applies a close-t target to the next return, while the original prompt requested execution at the following close. It also uses rounded public price curves. These figures are not comparable to the new common-date seasons and do not enter their rankings.

View common-date model seasons and frozen submissions

Why only forward results count

A language model has read a great deal about past markets, so it can write rules that look brilliant on history it already knows. A backtest of AI-written rules therefore proves little. The ranking below uses only returns after 2026-11-04, the day the rules were frozen: data no model could have seen. The historical backtest is shown further down for reference only.

The original prompt

Write a daily trading rule that uses only closing prices, is either long or in cash (no shorting, no leverage), trades at the next day's close, and that you expect to hold up across stocks, gold, oil, currencies and crypto over the next twelve months. Fix every parameter now; you will not be allowed to tune it later. Give it a name, a one-sentence idea and the exact rule.

The original experiment records these rules as Claude-authored in one pass without test results. It predates the new receipt and execution protocol. New providers participate in separate common-date seasons, not this legacy comparison.

The rules

AI-01 · Trend with a volatility brake

Ride the trend, but step aside when the market gets unusually turbulent.

Long when the close is above its 100-day average and 20-day volatility is below 1.5 times its one-year median; otherwise cash.

AI-03 · Buy the dip in an uptrend

Buy short pullbacks, but only in markets that are in a long-term uptrend.

Buy when the close is above its 200-day average but below its 10-day average; sell when it closes back above the 10-day average or falls below the 200-day average.

AI-04 · 50-day breakout, 20-day exit

Buy when the close is the highest of the last 50 days; sell when it closes below the lowest close of the previous 20 days.

AI-05 · Volatility-targeted trend

Follow the trend, but size the position so that its risk stays roughly constant.

When the close is above its 50-day average, hold a position of 15% divided by 20-day annualized volatility, capped at 100%; otherwise cash.

AI-06 · Stay invested, leave in a slump

Mostly stay invested, but get out of deep, still-falling drawdowns.

Stay long by default (first entry on a close above the 50-day average); sell only when the close is more than 15% below its one-year high and below the 50-day average; buy back when it closes above the 50-day average again.

How it is tested

  • Daily closing prices on nine markets: S&P 500, gold, silver, oil, broad commodities, agriculture, EUR/USD, bitcoin and ether. Natural gas is left out because its stored price history lacks the precision needed for daily returns.
  • A position is set at the close and earns the next session's return, as for every strategy on Quant Arena. Positions run from 0 (cash) to 100%; no shorting, no leverage.
  • Parameters are fixed. Nothing is optimized, now or later.
  • The median classic strategy is the median of our 72 walk-forward strategies on the same market over the same days.

An experiment, not investment advice. Results are before trading costs and will change every day.
