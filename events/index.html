Market event replays: who was already out, who got caught

For 35 major market events since 2010 we replayed our rule-based strategies on the affected market: which ones were already out of the market, which ones held on, and which made money. Prices and strategy curves run through 2026-11-09; results are before trading costs.

Which strategies lost less than the market, again and again

Across 29 sell-offs (every curated event where the market fell, each counted once on its primary market) the median market move was -18.8%. In the median event 98% of strategies lost less than the market, but only 5% finished with a gain. 37 of 62 strategies lost less than the market in every single event. Regime & other strategies protected best (median return -2.3%); Momentum strategies protected least (-9.4%).

How the events were measured

  • Researched dates are approximate. Each start is snapped to the highest close within 15 trading days of the researched start, and each end to the lowest close within 15 trading days of the researched end, after the start. Rallies use the mirror image (lowest close to highest close).
  • A move counts as meaningful only above a threshold: EUR/USD 3%; S&P 500, Gold 5%; Silver, Oil, Natural gas, Commodities, Agriculture 10%; Bitcoin, Ether 20%. Events below it, or on markets with no data yet, are removed (listed below).
  • Strategy return = change in the published equity curve from the first to the last day of the window. Before costs, cash earns nothing.
  • "Already out" is inferred, not recorded: a strategy counts as in the market if its equity moved on at least one of the last 3 trading days up to and including the window start, using data up to that day only.
  • "Lost less than the market" = the strategy's return in the window was higher than the market's. "Next 3 months" = the 63 trading days after the window end.
  • Only strategies with a curve on the event's market are replayed; portfolio, basket and multi-asset strategies without one are excluded, so the strategy count differs by market.
  • Automatic detection: a market is flagged when its close is at least the threshold below its trailing 60-day high. An episode ends at its trough once price has recovered a third of the fall or 20 trading days have passed since the trough. Episodes detected in the last 60 trading days that are not curated get an automatic page; curated events take precedence.

Researched events we removed

  • September 2019 Saudi Aramco attack spike: removed, the measured move of +1.0% on Oil is below the 10% threshold.
  • January 2025 DeepSeek sell-off: removed, the measured move of -2.0% on S&P 500 is below the 5% threshold.
  • 2010 Flash Crash: removed, it starts at the same peak on the same market as Spring 2010 correction (Greek debt crisis), which covers the whole decline.

The scanner also finds 420 older episodes in the price history that have not been written up; they are not shown.
