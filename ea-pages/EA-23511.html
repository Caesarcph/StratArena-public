Hedger

Hedger is an Expert Advisor that implements a hedging strategy by opening opposing positions to protect existing trades from adverse market movements. It monitors open positions and dynamically creates hedge orders when certain loss thresholds or market conditions are met, aiming to limit downside risk through correlated offsetting trades. The EA manages both the original and hedge positions with configurable parameters for hedge triggers and exit logic.

Strategy analysis

The core strategy of the Hedger EA is hedging, which automatically opens opposing positions when existing positions accumulate floating losses beyond a preset threshold. Entry conditions are triggered based on the floating loss amount or percentage of the open position, and when the threshold is exceeded, the EA opens a hedge position in the opposite direction or on a correlated instrument. Exit conditions typically involve closing the hedge position when it reaches a profit target or when the original position's risk is mitigated, sometimes closing both simultaneously. Position sizing for the hedge can be proportional to the original position or configured via user-defined parameters to control the hedge ratio. Money management relies on hedge ratio settings and maximum hedge count limits to control overall exposure. The strategy effectively limits directional risk but carries the danger that hedge positions can also accumulate losses, and in choppy markets both sides may lose.

Strengths

  • Effectively limits downside risk of directional positions, providing systematic risk protection
  • Automated hedging mechanism avoids human delay and responds quickly to adverse moves
  • Configurable hedge parameters offer flexibility to adapt to different market conditions
  • Hedge positions have independent profit targets and exit logic, not fully dependent on original positions

Weaknesses

  • In choppy markets, both original and hedge positions can incur losses simultaneously
  • Frequent hedging increases trading costs (spreads and commissions), potentially eroding profits
  • Hedging does not generate net profit but is purely a risk management tool, potentially masking underlying strategy flaws
  • If correlation between hedged instruments suddenly changes, hedge effectiveness can be significantly reduced

Possible improvements

  • Add volatility-based (ATR) dynamic hedge trigger thresholds instead of fixed percentages to adapt to different market volatility regimes
  • Introduce correlation monitoring mechanism that auto-adjusts hedge ratios or pauses hedging when instrument correlation weakens

Risk assessment

Overall risk: Medium

  • Dual-sided positions can simultaneously generate losses in extreme market conditions, increasing total account exposure
  • Lack of hard cap verification on hedge count could lead to over-hedging and insufficient margin
