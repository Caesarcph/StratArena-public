Neurotest

A simple feedforward neural network EA for MetaTrader 5 that uses a single hidden layer with ReLU activation to predict price direction from OHLCV data. It trains online during backtesting via momentum-based gradient descent, and opens buy/sell trades when the sigmoid output exceeds 0.6 or falls below 0.4 respectively. Risk management includes ATR-based dynamic stop loss, daily and total drawdown limits, and spread filtering.

Strategy analysis

The EA's strategy concept employs a simple 3-layer feedforward neural network (5 inputs, configurable hidden layer, 1 output) to learn price movement patterns from historical OHLCV candle data. Entry conditions are: buy when the sigmoid output exceeds 0.6, sell when it falls below 0.4, and no trade when between 0.4-0.6. Exit conditions rely on ATR-based dynamic stop loss and a 2:1 risk-reward take profit ratio, with no trailing stop or signal-based position closing logic. Position sizing is calculated from account equity and maximum risk percentage per trade, normalized to broker lot constraints including minimum/maximum lot and lot step. Money management enforces hard daily loss limit of 5% and total loss limit of 10%, with a penalty mechanism that reduces the learning rate when daily profit falls below the 1% target. However, the normalization logic has a critical flaw—computing min-max from a single sample forces all inputs to zero, and there is no check for existing positions in the same direction, which can lead to repeated trade entries on every tick.

Strengths

  • Implements neural network-based adaptive trading signal generation, conceptually innovative
  • Has a complete money management framework with daily/total drawdown limits and dynamic stop loss
  • Uses ATR-based dynamic stop loss to adapt to market volatility with a reasonable 1:2 risk-reward ratio
  • Model parameters can be persisted, allowing the EA to reuse trained weights after restart
  • Includes spread filter to avoid entering trades in high-spread environments

Weaknesses

  • Normalization logic is critically broken—computing min-max from a single sample normalizes all inputs to zero, making the network unable to learn effectively
  • No existing position check, potentially opening repeated trades every tick, leading to uncontrolled position accumulation
  • No new bar check means trading logic triggers every tick, causing redundant computation and potential duplicate orders
  • Network architecture is overly simplistic (5 raw price inputs + single hidden layer with 5 neurons), insufficient to capture meaningful market patterns
  • ATR indicator handle is created in GetDynamicStopLoss on every call without being released, causing resource leaks
  • Training uses raw price levels instead of differenced data or returns, lacking stationarity which hinders network generalization

Possible improvements

  • Fix normalization logic—use dataset-wide statistics (mean/std or global min-max) computed before training, and use differenced data or log returns instead of raw prices
  • Add position existence check and new bar check to prevent repeated entries and unnecessary per-tick computation; move ATR handle creation to OnInit and release in OnDeinit
  • Add validation set and early stopping to prevent overfitting; consider implementing a more robust optimizer (e.g., Adam) and more thoughtful network architecture design

Risk assessment

Overall risk: High risk

  • No position check allows repeated trade entries every tick, leading to uncontrolled position accumulation
  • Broken normalization logic makes neural network output meaningless, rendering trading decisions essentially random
  • ATR handle resource leak may exhaust indicator handles during extended operation
  • Training on raw prices instead of stationary series may cause the network to overfit to specific price levels
