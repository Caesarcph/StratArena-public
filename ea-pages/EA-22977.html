Secwenta

Secwenta is a trend-following Expert Advisor that opens trades when a configurable number of consecutive bullish or bearish bars appear, assuming that consecutive same-direction candles indicate trend continuation. It maintains a maximum of one open position at a time, operates only on new bar formation, and uses the WPR Custom indicator. BUY open signals simultaneously act as SELL close signals and vice versa, with optional direction-only trading modes.

Strategy analysis

Strategy concept: A trend-following strategy based on consecutive same-direction candles, with the core logic that when a specified number of consecutive bullish or bearish bars appear, the trend is expected to continue and the EA enters in that direction. Entry conditions: Open a long position when Number of bull bars consecutive bullish candles appear, open a short position when Number of bear bars consecutive bearish candles appear. Exit conditions: A BUY open signal simultaneously serves as a SELL close signal and vice versa; when trading only one direction, an opposite-direction single bar can serve as the close signal. Position sizing: Always maintains a maximum of one open position, compatible with both netting and hedging accounts. Money management: Position size controlled via lot size parameter, but there is no explicit stop loss, take profit, or percentage-based money management mechanism. The EA also integrates the WPR Custom indicator as an auxiliary signal filter.

Strengths

  • The strategy logic is extremely simple and clear, easy to understand and tune, suitable for beginners
  • Operates only on new bar formation, avoiding overtrading and excessive resource consumption from tick-by-tick processing
  • Always maintains a maximum of one open position, keeping risk exposure controlled, and is compatible with both netting and hedging account types
  • Supports buy-only or sell-only modes, providing high flexibility to adapt to different market views
  • The BUY open signal also serving as SELL close signal design gives the strategy a natural reversal mechanism

Weaknesses

  • Lacks explicit stop loss and take profit mechanisms, potentially facing large losses in volatile markets
  • Relies solely on consecutive candle count for trend detection, the signal is too simplistic and prone to frequent false breakouts in ranging markets
  • Does not account for spread, slippage, or transaction cost filtering, profits may be eroded by trading costs especially on short timeframes
  • Position sizing relies only on fixed lots, with no dynamic sizing based on account equity or volatility
  • The specific WPR indicator filtering rules are unclear; it may serve only as a visual display rather than a strict entry filter

Possible improvements

  • Add stop loss and take profit parameters, ideally ATR-based dynamic SL/TP to adapt to varying market volatility conditions
  • Add a trend filter (e.g., moving average direction, ADX) to avoid frequent opening in ranging/choppy markets
  • Incorporate spread filtering and trading session filtering to reduce high-cost trades and false signals during low-liquidity periods

Risk assessment

Overall risk: Medium

  • No stop loss mechanism, single trade loss can expand indefinitely
  • Consecutive candle signals frequently fail in ranging markets, potentially leading to repeated stop-outs or sustained floating losses
  • Fixed lot sizing without dynamic adjustment based on account equity poses over-leveraging risk
