Claim check: Time in the market beats timing

“Missing the best 10 days wrecks your returns, so time in the market beats timing the market.”

What the data says

Partly Partly: missing the best 10 days cost a median 35% of the total growth across 5 markets (0 at or above the one-third mark in both halves), and 60% of those best days fell within 21 trading days of one of the worst days. The direction mostly points the claimed way, but not strongly enough to be sure.

It holds in some markets or periods, leans the right way without proof, or helps on risk but not on return.

Who says it

Fund companies (JPMorgan, Fidelity) chart this in every retirement brochure.

Origins are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.

How we tested it

For each market with positive growth we replace the 10 best daily returns with zero (cash) and measure how much of the total log growth disappears; then the same for the 10 worst days, and for both. 'Wrecked' means at least a third of the total growth is lost, and in each half of the history the best 5 days must cost at least that share. We also count how many of the best days fall within 21 trading days of one of the worst days.

The honest caveat

The exercise is hypothetical (nobody can pick those days) and it also shows missing the worst days helps just as much.

Descriptive statistics from historical data, before costs. Not a forecast and not investment advice.
