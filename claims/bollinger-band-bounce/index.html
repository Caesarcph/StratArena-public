Claim check: Bollinger Band bounce

“Price touching the lower Bollinger Band is a buy; upper band is a sell.”

What the data says

Does not hold Not supported by the data: after “Close below the lower band”, the average 10-day return was -0.1% against +0.2% for all days (medians over 10 markets); it was higher than usual in 5 of 10 markets and statistically clear, with at least 20 independent events and the same sign in both halves, in 0. Results are mixed across markets and cannot be told apart from chance.

No effect we can tell from chance, or the opposite effect.

Who says it

John Bollinger's bands (1980s); every retail trading tutorial.

Origins are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.

How we tested it

Bands are the 20-day average plus and minus two sample standard deviations of the close. Event: the close is below the lower band. We record the 10-trading-day return that follows (events at least 10 days apart) and compare it with the market's average 10-day return. The upper-band half is checked the same way (returns should be lower than average).
