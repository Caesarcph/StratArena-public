Claim check: Dollar-cost averaging vs lump sum

“Dollar-cost averaging beats investing a lump sum.”

What the data says

Does not hold Not supported by the data: after twelve months, dollar-cost averaging ended ahead of a lump sum on a median 50% of start dates across 10 markets, and lump sum was ahead on more than 60% of start dates in 3 of them; the 5th-percentile outcome was -19% for DCA and -30% for the lump sum (medians over markets). It does not win more often than a coin flip, though it softens the worst outcomes.

No effect we can tell from chance, or the opposite effect.

Who says it

Vanguard's research says lump sum wins 2/3 of time; DCA is nonetheless an everyday recommendation.

Origins are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.

How we tested it

For every possible start date: a lump sum buys everything on day 0; dollar-cost averaging buys one twelfth on day 0 and then one twelfth every month for 11 more months, with the rest in cash at 0%. Both are valued twelve months after the start. We report the share of start dates on which DCA ends ahead, and the 5th-percentile outcome of each. Start dates overlap heavily, so they are not independent trials.
