Claim check: The 200-day moving average rule

“Only be invested when price is above the 200-day moving average.”

What the data says

Partly Partly: the rule gave a maximum drawdown at least a quarter smaller together with a higher Sharpe ratio in 6 of 10 markets over 2010-10-07 to 2026-11-12 and in both halves of the history in 3; the median CAGR was +4.3% against +3.2% for buy-and-hold and the median maximum drawdown -51.2% against -71.2% (before costs). It holds in some markets, not across the board.

It holds in some markets or periods, leans the right way without proof, or helps on risk but not on return.

Who says it

Mebane Faber's 2007 'A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation' and Paul Tudor Jones's '200-day MA' remark made it a mainstream rule.

Origins are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.

How we tested it

Long from the day after the close is above its 200-day simple average, cash from the day after it closes below. Compared with buy-and-hold over the same dates. The rule counts as working only if it cuts the maximum drawdown by at least a quarter and also improves the Sharpe ratio, in the full history and in both halves.
