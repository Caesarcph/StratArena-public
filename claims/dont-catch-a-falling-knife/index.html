Claim check: Do not catch a falling knife

“Don't catch a falling knife: never buy something in a downtrend.”

What the data says

Partly Partly: after “20-day fall of 10% or more, below the 200-day average”, the average 21-day return was -0.7% against +0.6% for all days (medians over 6 markets); it was lower than usual in 5 of 6 markets and statistically clear, with at least 20 independent events and the same sign in both halves, in 0. The direction mostly points the claimed way, but not strongly enough to be sure.

It holds in some markets or periods, leans the right way without proof, or helps on risk but not on return.

Who says it

Trader's proverb contradicting 'buy the dip'.

Origins are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.

How we tested it

Event: the close is down 10% or more over 20 trading days and is below its 200-day average. We record the 21-trading-day return from that close (events at least 21 days apart). The claim says buying here is a bad idea, so it holds only if the return is clearly lower than the market's average 21-day return. The same fall above the 200-day average is shown for contrast.
