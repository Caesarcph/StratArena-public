Claim check: RSI 30/70 overbought and oversold

“Buy when RSI drops below 30 (oversold), sell when it rises above 70 (overbought).”

What the data says

Does not hold Not supported by the data: after “RSI(14) below 30”, the average 10-day return was +0.1% against +0.2% for all days (medians over 10 markets); it was higher than usual in 5 of 10 markets and statistically clear, with at least 20 independent events and the same sign in both halves, in 0. Results are mixed across markets and cannot be told apart from chance.

No effect we can tell from chance, or the opposite effect.

Who says it

J. Welles Wilder Jr., 'New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems' (1978); the most-quoted indicator rule on YouTube and Reddit.

Origins are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.

How we tested it

Wilder RSI(14) of the close. Event: the close at which RSI is below 30. We record the 10-trading-day return that follows (events at least 10 days apart) and compare it with the average 10-day return over all days of the same market. The sell half is checked the same way: returns after RSI above 70 should be lower than average. A full buy-under-30, sell-over-70 rule (maximum 20 days held) is shown as a cross-check.
