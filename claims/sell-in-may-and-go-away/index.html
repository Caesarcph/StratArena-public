Claim check: Sell in May and go away

“Sell in May and go away: stocks do better from November to April than May to October.”

What the data says

Does not hold Not supported by the data: May to October returned +1.0% a month on average against +1.4% for the other months in S&P 500 (n = 102 months, 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-10; t = -0.7, likely noise). The direction points the claimed way, but the result cannot be told apart from chance.

No effect we can tell from chance, or the opposite effect.

Who says it

Old London stock-market adage, 'Halloween indicator' popularised by Bouman and Jacobsen (2002).

Origins are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.

How we tested it

Month-end to month-end returns for the S&P 500 (SPY). Summer months (May to October) are compared with winter months (November to April) with a difference-of-means t-test, and the sign must be the same in both halves of the history. The claim holds only if summer is lower than winter and the gap passes the significance guard.
