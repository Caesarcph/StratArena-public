Claim check: Cut losses, let profits run

“Cut your losses short and let your profits run: use a stop loss.”

What the data says

Does not hold Not supported by the data: the rule gave a maximum drawdown at least a quarter smaller together with a higher Sharpe ratio in 1 of 10 markets over 2010-01-01 to 2026-11-11 and in both halves of the history in 1; the median CAGR was +3.3% against +4.1% for buy-and-hold and the median maximum drawdown -58.7% against -71.2% (before costs). Most markets do not show the claimed benefit.

No effect we can tell from chance, or the opposite effect.

Who says it

David Ricardo's 19th-century rule, repeated by Jesse Livermore and every risk-management course.

Origins are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.

How we tested it

Hold the market; if the close falls 15% below the highest close since entry (a trailing stop, which is what 'let profits run' implies), go to cash; re-enter when the close exceeds its previous 50-day high. Compared with buy-and-hold. The stop counts as working only if it cuts the maximum drawdown by at least a quarter and also improves the Sharpe ratio, in the full history and in both halves. Tighter and looser trailing stops and a fixed stop 10% below the entry price are shown as robustness checks. Stops are tested on daily closes, so gaps are not modelled.
