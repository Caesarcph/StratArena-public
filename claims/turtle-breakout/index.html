Claim check: Turtle breakouts

“Buy 20-day or 55-day breakouts, the turtle way.”

What the data says

Partly Partly: the rule gave a higher Sharpe ratio than buy-and-hold in 4 of 10 markets over 2010-01-29 to 2026-11-10 and in both halves of the history in 2; the median CAGR was +3.0% against +4.5% for buy-and-hold and the median maximum drawdown -44.8% against -71.2% (before costs). It holds in some markets, not across the board.

It holds in some markets or periods, leans the right way without proof, or helps on risk but not on return.

Who says it

Richard Dennis and Bill Eckhardt's Turtle experiment (1983).

Origins are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.

How we tested it

Long from the day after the close exceeds the highest close of the previous 20 days; cash from the day after the close falls below the lowest close of the previous 10 days. The claim holds if the Sharpe ratio beats buy-and-hold in the full history and in both halves on most markets. The original Turtle system used intraday highs and lows, so this is a close-based approximation.
