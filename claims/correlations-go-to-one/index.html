Claim check: Correlations go to one in a crisis

“In a crisis all correlations go to 1, so diversification fails when you need it.”

What the data says

Partly Partly: the average pairwise correlation among the ten markets was 0.25 while SPY was more than 10% below its peak, against 0.16 otherwise, a rise of +0.09 (-0.01 in the first half, +0.13 in the second). The link is real but weaker than the saying implies.

It holds in some markets or periods, leans the right way without proof, or helps on risk but not on return.

Who says it

Common refrain after 2008, 2020.

Origins are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.

How we tested it

For every pair of the ten markets we compute the correlation of daily returns on common dates, separately for days when SPY is more than 10% below its previous peak (stress, known at the previous close) and for all other days, then average over pairs. A rise of at least 0.15 in both halves of the history counts as correlations rising meaningfully in stress.
