Claim check: Golden cross and death cross

“A golden cross (50-day MA crossing above the 200-day) is bullish; a death cross is bearish.”

What the data says

Partly Partly: days spent in the position earned more than days outside it in 8 of 10 markets (2010-10-07 to 2026-11-10); the gap was statistically clear and had the same sign in both halves in 0, and the median gap was +7.8% a year. The direction mostly points the claimed way, but not strongly enough to be sure.

It holds in some markets or periods, leans the right way without proof, or helps on risk but not on return.

Who says it

Chart-pattern staple on CNBC and Investopedia, repeated every time the S&P 500 prints a cross.

Origins are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.

How we tested it

We hold the market from the day after the 50-day average closes above the 200-day average (golden state) and stay out from the day after it closes below (death state). The claim says the golden state is bullish, so the test asks whether days spent in the golden state earned more than days spent outside it (difference of means, Welch t-stat), and whether that holds in both halves of each market's history. Separately we look at the 63-trading-day return after each cross itself.
