Claim checker

Do popular trading sayings hold up? We tested 34 of them

Golden cross, sell in May, buy the dip, cut your losses: each saying is turned into a precise rule, run on up to 16 years of daily data for ten markets, and given one of four verdicts. Data through 2026-11-13.

The scorecard

  • Holds 1 The effect shows up in most markets, passes the significance guard, and has the same sign in both halves of the history.
  • Partly 11 It holds in some markets or periods, leans the right way without proof, or helps on risk but not on return.
  • Does not hold 19 No effect we can tell from chance, or the opposite effect.
  • Not enough data 3 Too few independent events or observations to judge either way.

How we judge

Every saying is written as an exact rule or condition. Rules are long-or-cash on daily closes: a position decided at the close of day t earns the return of day t+1, indicators use only data up to day t, results are before trading costs, and cash earns nothing. Event studies compare returns after a condition with the average for the same market, using non-overlapping events.

A result counts as statistically visible only with |t| of at least 2 and at least 20 independent events; anything weaker is labelled likely noise. Every test is split into two halves of the market's history and a claim can only hold if both halves agree. A claim holds when at least 60% of the usable markets show the effect; it is partly true when only some do, when the direction is right in at least 70% of five or more markets without proof, or when it helps on risk but not on return. With fewer than 10 events a market is not usable, and when fewer than half of the markets are usable the verdict is not enough data.

Prices are daily closes of ten markets (S&P 500, gold, silver, oil, natural gas, commodities, agriculture, EUR/USD, Bitcoin, Ether) from 2010 (Bitcoin 2014, Ether 2017). Several claims are about stocks, and we have one stock market, SPY, so they are decided on SPY alone and the other markets are shown for context. The page sources of each saying are as commonly cited and were not independently verified.
