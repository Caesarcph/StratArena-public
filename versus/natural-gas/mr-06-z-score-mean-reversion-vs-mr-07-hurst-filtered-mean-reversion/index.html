Z-Score Mean Reversion vs Hurst-Filtered Mean Reversion on Natural gas: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-10: 4240 trading days (16.8 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of Natural gas are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

Z-Score Mean Reversion had the higher CAGR (+0.3% vs -2.6% for Hurst-Filtered Mean Reversion). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.08 vs -0.01, a gap under 0.1. Z-Score Mean Reversion had the shallower maximum drawdown (-23.0% vs -60.9%). Both beat buy-and-hold of Natural gas on CAGR (-25.1%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.12. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR -0.6% with a Sharpe of 0.00 and a maximum drawdown of -36.0%. A correlation below 0.3 means the two often did different things, which is the case where mixing helps most. The mix did not beat the better strategy on Sharpe (0.00 vs 0.08).
