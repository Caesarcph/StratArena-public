Z-Score Mean Reversion vs Crisis Alpha on Natural gas: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-23: 4249 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of Natural gas are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

Crisis Alpha had the higher CAGR (+3.5% vs +0.3% for Z-Score Mean Reversion). On risk-adjusted return Crisis Alpha led, with a Sharpe of 0.31 against 0.08. Z-Score Mean Reversion had the shallower maximum drawdown (-23.0% vs -84.7%). Both beat buy-and-hold of Natural gas on CAGR (-25.3%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was -0.06. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +4.8% with a Sharpe of 0.32 and a maximum drawdown of -56.7%. A correlation below 0.3 means the two often did different things, which is the case where mixing helps most. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.32 vs 0.31).
