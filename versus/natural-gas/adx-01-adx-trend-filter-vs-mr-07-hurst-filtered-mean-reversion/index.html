ADX Trend Filter vs Hurst-Filtered Mean Reversion on Natural gas: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-13: 4243 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of Natural gas are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

Hurst-Filtered Mean Reversion had the higher CAGR (-2.6% vs -3.1% for ADX Trend Filter). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe -0.02 vs -0.01, a gap under 0.1. ADX Trend Filter had the shallower maximum drawdown (-52.0% vs -60.9%). Both beat buy-and-hold of Natural gas on CAGR (-25.2%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.01. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR -1.6% with a Sharpe of -0.02 and a maximum drawdown of -44.9%. A correlation below 0.3 means the two often did different things, which is the case where mixing helps most. The mix did not beat the better strategy on Sharpe (-0.02 vs -0.01).
