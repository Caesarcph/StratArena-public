Crisis Alpha vs Volatility Regime Strategy on Natural gas: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-25: 4251 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of Natural gas are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

Crisis Alpha had the higher CAGR (+3.2% vs -0.5% for Volatility Regime Strategy). On risk-adjusted return Crisis Alpha led, with a Sharpe of 0.30 against 0.09. Volatility Regime Strategy had the shallower maximum drawdown (-53.9% vs -84.7%). Both beat buy-and-hold of Natural gas on CAGR (-25.1%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was -0.50. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +6.3% with a Sharpe of 0.40 and a maximum drawdown of -39.2%. A correlation below 0.3 means the two often did different things, which is the case where mixing helps most. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.40 vs 0.30).
