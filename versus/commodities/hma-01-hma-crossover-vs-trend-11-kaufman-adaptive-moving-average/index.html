HMA Crossover vs Kaufman Adaptive Moving Average on Commodities: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-10: 4240 trading days (16.8 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of Commodities are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

The two had about the same CAGR (+5.1% for HMA Crossover, +5.2% for Kaufman Adaptive Moving Average). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.48 vs 0.53, a gap under 0.1. Kaufman Adaptive Moving Average had the shallower maximum drawdown (-28.0% vs -33.2%). Both beat buy-and-hold of Commodities on CAGR (+2.2%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.61. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +5.3% with a Sharpe of 0.56 and a maximum drawdown of -21.4%. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.56 vs 0.53).
