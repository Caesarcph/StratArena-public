MACD + RSI Filter vs Kaufman Adaptive Moving Average on Commodities: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-11: 4241 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of Commodities are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

The two had about the same CAGR (+4.8% for MACD + RSI Filter, +5.2% for Kaufman Adaptive Moving Average). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.55 vs 0.53, a gap under 0.1. Kaufman Adaptive Moving Average had the shallower maximum drawdown (-28.0% vs -31.0%). Both beat buy-and-hold of Commodities on CAGR (+2.2%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.49. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +5.2% with a Sharpe of 0.62 and a maximum drawdown of -22.6%. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.62 vs 0.55).
