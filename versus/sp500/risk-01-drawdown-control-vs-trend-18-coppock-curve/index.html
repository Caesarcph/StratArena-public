Drawdown Control vs Coppock Curve on S&P 500: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-11: 4241 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of S&P 500 are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

Drawdown Control had the higher CAGR (+11.4% vs +8.1% for Coppock Curve). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.80 vs 0.79, a gap under 0.1. Coppock Curve had the shallower maximum drawdown (-18.2% vs -28.0%). Neither beat buy-and-hold of S&P 500 on CAGR (+13.9%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.66. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +9.9% with a Sharpe of 0.87 and a maximum drawdown of -18.9%. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.87 vs 0.80).
