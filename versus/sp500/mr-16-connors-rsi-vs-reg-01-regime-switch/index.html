Connors RSI vs Regime Switch on S&P 500: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-10: 4240 trading days (16.8 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of S&P 500 are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

Regime Switch had the higher CAGR (+9.9% vs +6.0% for Connors RSI). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.80 vs 0.90, a gap under 0.1. Maximum drawdowns were similar (-16.8% vs -17.7%). Neither beat buy-and-hold of S&P 500 on CAGR (+13.8%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.22. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +8.1% with a Sharpe of 1.09 and a maximum drawdown of -12.0%. A correlation below 0.3 means the two often did different things, which is the case where mixing helps most. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (1.09 vs 0.90).
