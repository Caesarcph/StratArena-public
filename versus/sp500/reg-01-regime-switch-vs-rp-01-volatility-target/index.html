Regime Switch vs Volatility Target on S&P 500: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-12: 4242 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of S&P 500 are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

Volatility Target had the higher CAGR (+10.6% vs +9.9% for Regime Switch). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.91 vs 0.92, a gap under 0.1. Volatility Target had the shallower maximum drawdown (-16.6% vs -17.7%). Neither beat buy-and-hold of S&P 500 on CAGR (+13.9%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.83. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +10.3% with a Sharpe of 0.96 and a maximum drawdown of -15.0%. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.96 vs 0.92).
