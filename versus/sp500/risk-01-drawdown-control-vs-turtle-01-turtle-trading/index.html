Drawdown Control vs Turtle Trading on S&P 500: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-10: 4240 trading days (16.8 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of S&P 500 are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

Drawdown Control had the higher CAGR (+11.3% vs +6.7% for Turtle Trading). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.80 vs 0.78, a gap under 0.1. Turtle Trading had the shallower maximum drawdown (-13.3% vs -28.0%). Neither beat buy-and-hold of S&P 500 on CAGR (+13.8%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.55. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +9.2% with a Sharpe of 0.89 and a maximum drawdown of -18.0%. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.89 vs 0.80).
