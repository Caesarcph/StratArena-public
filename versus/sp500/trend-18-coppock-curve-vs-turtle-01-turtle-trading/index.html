Coppock Curve vs Turtle Trading on S&P 500: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-12: 4242 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of S&P 500 are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

Coppock Curve had the higher CAGR (+8.1% vs +6.7% for Turtle Trading). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.79 vs 0.78, a gap under 0.1. Turtle Trading had the shallower maximum drawdown (-13.3% vs -18.2%). Neither beat buy-and-hold of S&P 500 on CAGR (+13.9%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.81. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +7.5% with a Sharpe of 0.83 and a maximum drawdown of -15.6%. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.83 vs 0.79).
