Divergence Strategy vs HMA Crossover on Silver: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-20: 4248 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of Silver are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

HMA Crossover had the higher CAGR (+9.3% vs +7.6% for Divergence Strategy). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.43 vs 0.50, a gap under 0.1. HMA Crossover had the shallower maximum drawdown (-53.9% vs -58.7%). Both beat buy-and-hold of Silver on CAGR (+4.6%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.57. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +9.1% with a Sharpe of 0.53 and a maximum drawdown of -55.6%. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.53 vs 0.50).
