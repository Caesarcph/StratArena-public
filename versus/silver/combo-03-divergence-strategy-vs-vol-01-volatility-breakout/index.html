Divergence Strategy vs Volatility Breakout on Silver: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-25: 4251 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of Silver are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

Divergence Strategy had the higher CAGR (+7.6% vs +4.8% for Volatility Breakout). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.43 vs 0.46, a gap under 0.1. Volatility Breakout had the shallower maximum drawdown (-20.5% vs -58.8%). Both beat buy-and-hold of Silver on CAGR (+4.6%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.26. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +6.9% with a Sharpe of 0.54 and a maximum drawdown of -31.9%. A correlation below 0.3 means the two often did different things, which is the case where mixing helps most. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.54 vs 0.46).
