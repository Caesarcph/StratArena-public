Volatility Regime Switch vs Coppock Curve on Gold: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-25: 4251 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of Gold are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

Coppock Curve had the higher CAGR (+6.7% vs +4.5% for Volatility Regime Switch). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.52 vs 0.58, a gap under 0.1. Coppock Curve had the shallower maximum drawdown (-22.2% vs -29.4%). Only Coppock Curve beat buy-and-hold of Gold on CAGR (+6.7%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.67. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +5.7% with a Sharpe of 0.61 and a maximum drawdown of -24.9%. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.61 vs 0.58).
