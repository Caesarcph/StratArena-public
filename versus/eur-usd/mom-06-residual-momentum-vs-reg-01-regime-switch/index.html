Residual Momentum vs Regime Switch on EUR/USD: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-01 to 2026-11-11: 4389 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of EUR/USD are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

The two had about the same CAGR (+0.2% for Residual Momentum, +0.2% for Regime Switch). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.06 vs 0.07, a gap under 0.1. Residual Momentum had the shallower maximum drawdown (-13.5% vs -14.6%). Both beat buy-and-hold of EUR/USD on CAGR (-1.5%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.63. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +0.2% with a Sharpe of 0.07 and a maximum drawdown of -13.0%. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.07 vs 0.07).
