Residual Momentum vs Connors RSI on EUR/USD: which did better?

Common history 2010-01-01 to 2026-11-26: 4400 trading days (16.9 years). Both strategies and buy-and-hold of EUR/USD are measured over exactly this window, before trading costs.

Verdict from the numbers

The two had about the same CAGR (+0.2% for Residual Momentum, +0.1% for Connors RSI). On risk-adjusted return it is too close to call: Sharpe 0.06 vs 0.04, a gap under 0.1. Connors RSI had the shallower maximum drawdown (-11.1% vs -13.5%). Both beat buy-and-hold of EUR/USD on CAGR (-1.6%). This describes past results over this window, before costs; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Correlation and a 50/50 mix

The daily-return correlation of the two strategies was 0.18. Holding half of each, rebalanced daily, would have returned CAGR +0.2% with a Sharpe of 0.07 and a maximum drawdown of -9.2%. A correlation below 0.3 means the two often did different things, which is the case where mixing helps most. The mix had a higher Sharpe than either strategy alone (0.07 vs 0.06).
