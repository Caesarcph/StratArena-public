Bitcoin HODL: does it still work? Replicated through 2026

We re-ran it on Bitcoin from 2014-09-17 to 2026-11-12 (12.2 years, before costs): +51.5% a year, Sharpe 0.96, worst drawdown -83.4%. The benchmark, Bitcoin buy-and-hold, did +51.5% a year (Sharpe 0.96, worst drawdown -83.4%).

Verdict: About the same risk-adjusted return as its benchmark. Historical, hypothetical results before costs, taxes and fees. Not investment advice.

Rule as published Rule as published

This replica applies the published rule as written, on the instrument we have. It is still a backtest on ETF data, before costs.

What is lost

  • Bitcoin's earliest years (before 2014-09) are not in the data.

Where it comes from

Attributed toAnonymous BitcoinTalk poster 'GameKyuubi' (originator of the term)
Source'I AM HODLING' BitcoinTalk forum post (2013)

The crypto community's defining mantra and the benchmark every crypto strategy has to beat.

Rules, weights and thresholds are given as commonly cited. We have not re-checked them against the primary text.

The original rules

Buy bitcoin and hold through volatility without trading.

How we replicate it

What we changed

100% BTC-USD from the first available date (2014-09), never rebalanced. History starts only in 2014, so the huge early Bitcoin gains are not in the sample.

The exact rule we run

Buy 100% BTC-USD on the first day of data and hold it. No rebalancing, no trading.

Target weights: 100.0% Bitcoin.

Conventions: Position decided at a close earns the next day's return; no look-ahead; before costs; cash earns 0%; starts 2014-09-17, when all its assets have data; weights drift between rebalances (never rebalanced); rebalanced at the last close of the period.

How much to trust it

  • A fixed allocation has no signals, so it is one path of history (12.2 years), not a test.
  • This is one backtest on one path of history, before costs and with hindsight on the choice of strategy. It does not show the strategy will work in future.

Honest caveat

A faithful replica of a passive idea, but the origin of the term (a 2013 forum post) is given as commonly cited.
