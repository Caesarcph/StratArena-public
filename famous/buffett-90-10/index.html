Buffett 90/10 Portfolio: does it still work? Replicated through 2026

We re-ran it on S&P 500 from 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-11 (16.9 years, before costs): +12.6% a year, Sharpe 0.85, worst drawdown -30.5%. The benchmark, S&P 500 buy-and-hold, did +13.9% a year (Sharpe 0.85, worst drawdown -33.7%).

Verdict: About the same risk-adjusted return as its benchmark. Historical, hypothetical results before costs, taxes and fees. Not investment advice.

Adapted Adapted: this is not the real thing

This is our adapted version, not the real portfolio or fund. Read what is lost before comparing it with anything published about the original.

What is lost

  • The 10% short-term government bond sleeve is cash earning 0% instead of a short-term yield.
Bond sleeve held as cash

10% of the original allocation is government bonds. We have no bond data, so that 10% is held as cash earning 0%.

What this means: no coupon income, no price gains when yields fall or stocks crash, and none of the losses bonds had when yields rose (2022). Read the results as the non-bond part plus idle cash. The real portfolio would have done differently, better in some years and worse in others.

Where it comes from

Attributed toWarren Buffett
SourceBerkshire Hathaway 2013 Annual Letter to Shareholders (2014)

Buffett's own stated recommendation for ordinary investors' retirement money.

Rules, weights and thresholds are given as commonly cited. We have not re-checked them against the primary text.

The original rules

Instructions for his wife's trust: 10% in short-term government bonds and 90% in a very low-cost S&P 500 index fund.

How we replicate it

What we changed

Short-term government bonds are replaced by cash; SPY stands in for the S&P 500 index fund.

The exact rule we run

Buy 90% SPY and keep 10% in cash (0%). Rebalance at the last close of each year.

Target weights: 90.0% S&P 500, 10.0% Cash (0%).

Conventions: Position decided at a close earns the next day's return; no look-ahead; before costs; cash earns 0%; starts 2010-01-04, when all its assets have data; weights drift between rebalances (annually); rebalanced at the last close of the period.

How much to trust it

  • A fixed allocation has no signals, so it is one path of history (16.9 years), not a test.
  • This is one backtest on one path of history, before costs and with hindsight on the choice of strategy. It does not show the strategy will work in future.

Honest caveat

Very close to the original; the 10% sleeve earns 0% instead of short-term bond yield.
