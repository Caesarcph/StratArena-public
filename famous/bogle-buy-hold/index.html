Bogle Buy-and-Hold Index Fund: does it still work? Replicated through 2026

We re-ran it on S&P 500 from 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-10 (16.8 years, before costs): +13.8% a year, Sharpe 0.85, worst drawdown -33.7%. The benchmark, S&P 500 buy-and-hold, did +13.8% a year (Sharpe 0.85, worst drawdown -33.7%).

Verdict: About the same risk-adjusted return as its benchmark. Historical, hypothetical results before costs, taxes and fees. Not investment advice.

Rule as published Rule as published

This replica applies the published rule as written, on the instrument we have. It is still a backtest on ETF data, before costs.

What is lost

  • Bogle suggested holding bonds in proportion to age; this is the stock-only core (100% SPY).

Where it comes from

Attributed toJohn C. Bogle
SourceThe Little Book of Common Sense Investing (2007)

Bogle founded Vanguard and the first index fund, making buy-and-hold the benchmark every other strategy is judged against.

Rules, weights and thresholds are given as commonly cited. We have not re-checked them against the primary text.

The original rules

Buy a low-cost, broad market index fund and hold it forever, ignoring market timing; Bogle suggested adding bonds by age, but the core idea is the equity index fund.

How we replicate it

What we changed

100% SPY, never rebalanced; this is the passive benchmark and the bond component Bogle suggested for older investors is omitted.

The exact rule we run

Buy 100% SPY on the first day and hold it. No rebalancing, no trading.

Target weights: 100.0% S&P 500.

Conventions: Position decided at a close earns the next day's return; no look-ahead; before costs; cash earns 0%; starts 2010-01-04, when all its assets have data; weights drift between rebalances (never rebalanced); rebalanced at the last close of the period.

How much to trust it

  • A fixed allocation has no signals, so it is one path of history (16.8 years), not a test.
  • This is one backtest on one path of history, before costs and with hindsight on the choice of strategy. It does not show the strategy will work in future.

Honest caveat

A faithful stock-only version; the original lets investors hold a bond share by age.
