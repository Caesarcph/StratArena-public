Livermore Pivotal-Point Breakout (simplified): does it still work? Replicated through 2026

We re-ran it on 10 markets from 2010-03-26 to 2026-11-10 (16.6 years, before costs): +5.7% a year, Sharpe 0.76, worst drawdown -23.8%. The benchmark, Equal-weight of 10 markets, did +9.6% a year (Sharpe 0.64, worst drawdown -43.1%).

Verdict: Smoother ride than its benchmark (higher Sharpe), but a lower return. Historical, hypothetical results before costs, taxes and fees. Not investment advice.

Our interpretation Our interpretation: not a replica

The originator never published a numeric rule. The parameters here are ours, an illustration of the idea. Do not read these results as the originator's results.

What is lost

  • The 60-day window and the 10% stop are our parameters, not Livermore's.
  • Pyramiding, tape reading, judgement about the market and shorting.

Where it comes from

Attributed toJesse Livermore
SourceHow to Trade in Stocks (Livermore 1940); Reminiscences of a Stock Operator (Lefevre 1923) (1940)

Livermore is the archetypal speculator whose boom-and-bust life inspired generations of traders.

Rules, weights and thresholds are given as commonly cited. We have not re-checked them against the primary text.

The original rules

Buy when price breaks out of a long consolidation through a 'pivotal point' (a prior resistance), add only as it proves right, and cut losses quickly instead of averaging down.

How we replicate it

What we changed

Livermore never published exact numbers. This is OUR illustration of his method: buy when the close breaks above its prior 60-day closing high, sell when it falls 10% below the highest close since entry, with a fixed 1/10 slot per market. Pyramiding, tape reading and shorts are dropped.

The exact rule we run

For each of the 10 markets, at the close: when flat and the close is above the highest close of the prior 60 days, go long; exit to cash when the close is below 90% of the highest close since entry. Each market has a fixed 1/10 slot.

Conventions: Position decided at a close earns the next day's return; no look-ahead; before costs; cash earns 0%; starts 2010-03-26, the first day a signal exists; every market has an equal slot of equity; indicators use each market's own trading days.

Growth of 1 vs the benchmark

Benchmark: Equal-weight of 10 markets, same period. Equal slots in S&P 500, Gold, Silver, Oil, Natural gas, Commodities, Agriculture, EUR/USD, Bitcoin, Ether, rebalanced daily; a market without data yet stays in cash.
