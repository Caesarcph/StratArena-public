Paul Tudor Jones 200-Day Moving Average: does it still work? Replicated through 2026

We re-ran it on S&P 500 from 2010-10-18 to 2026-11-11 (16.1 years, before costs): +10.6% a year, Sharpe 0.92, worst drawdown -19.8%. The benchmark, S&P 500 buy-and-hold, did +14.3% a year (Sharpe 0.87, worst drawdown -33.7%).

Verdict: About the same risk-adjusted return as its benchmark. Historical, hypothetical results before costs, taxes and fees. Not investment advice.

Our interpretation Our interpretation: not a replica

The originator never published a numeric rule. The parameters here are ours, an illustration of the idea. Do not read these results as the originator's results.

What is lost

  • Jones trades discretionarily across markets; a single mechanical rule on SPY is not his strategy.
  • The quote's exact wording and source are unverified.

Rules, weights and thresholds are given as commonly cited. We have not re-checked them against the primary text.

The original rules

Jones has said that he uses the 200-day moving average of closing prices as a basic risk gauge: if price is below it, stay out or reduce.

How we replicate it

What we changed

Our interpretation of a widely quoted remark, not a rule he published: hold SPY while its close is above its 200-day average, otherwise cash. The exact wording and source of the quote are not verified.

The exact rule we run

Every day at the close: if the SPY close is above the average of its last 200 closes, hold 100% SPY, otherwise 100% cash.

Conventions: Position decided at a close earns the next day's return; no look-ahead; before costs; cash earns 0%; starts 2010-10-18, the first day a signal exists; every market has an equal slot of equity; indicators use each market's own trading days.
