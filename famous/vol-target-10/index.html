Volatility-Managed SPY (10% target): does it still work? Replicated through 2026

We re-ran it on S&P 500 from 2010-02-02 to 2026-11-09 (16.8 years, before costs): +9.6% a year, Sharpe 0.95, worst drawdown -12.8%. The benchmark, S&P 500 buy-and-hold, did +14.1% a year (Sharpe 0.86, worst drawdown -33.7%).

Verdict: About the same risk-adjusted return as its benchmark. Historical, hypothetical results before costs, taxes and fees. Not investment advice.

Adapted Adapted: this is not the real thing

This is our adapted version, not the real portfolio or fund. Read what is lost before comparing it with anything published about the original.

What is lost

  • The paper's monthly realised-variance scaling and its factor portfolios.
  • No leverage: in calm markets the original would run above 100%.

Where it comes from

Attributed toAlan Moreira & Tyler Muir
SourceVolatility-Managed Portfolios, Journal of Finance (2017)

A highly cited paper showing that risk scaling beats plain buy-and-hold, the basis of risk-managed funds.

Rules, weights and thresholds are given as commonly cited. We have not re-checked them against the primary text.

The original rules

Scale the exposure to a risk factor inversely to its recent realized variance, holding less when volatility is high, which raised risk-adjusted returns; this is the basis of volatility targeting.

How we replicate it

What we changed

The paper scales a factor by the inverse of last month's realised variance. We use a simpler daily version: the SPY weight is 10% divided by the annualised 20-day volatility, capped at 100% (no leverage), the rest in cash.

The exact rule we run

Every day at the close: sigma = standard deviation of the last 20 SPY daily returns times the square root of 252. SPY weight = the smaller of 100% and 10% / sigma; the rest is cash.

Conventions: Position decided at a close earns the next day's return; no look-ahead; before costs; cash earns 0%; starts 2010-02-02, the first day a signal exists; every market has an equal slot of equity; indicators use each market's own trading days.

How much to trust it

  • 2978 position changes over 16.8 years.
  • This is one backtest on one path of history, before costs and with hindsight on the choice of strategy. It does not show the strategy will work in future.

Honest caveat

The paper's exact method and the 10% target are not identical; this is the commonly used practical implementation.
