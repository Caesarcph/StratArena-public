O'Neil 52-Week-High Breakout with 8% Stop: does it still work? Replicated through 2026

We re-ran it on 10 markets from 2010-12-21 to 2026-11-11 (15.9 years, before costs): +10.1% a year, Sharpe 0.85, worst drawdown -26.5%. The benchmark, Equal-weight of 10 markets, did +9.2% a year (Sharpe 0.62, worst drawdown -43.1%).

Verdict: Better risk-adjusted return than its benchmark over this period. Historical, hypothetical results before costs, taxes and fees. Not investment advice.

Adapted Adapted: this is not the real thing

This is our adapted version, not the real portfolio or fund. Read what is lost before comparing it with anything published about the original.

What is lost

  • CAN SLIM stock picking (earnings, leaders, volume, market direction) is the heart of the method and is absent.
  • No base-pattern recognition: any new 252-day high counts.

Where it comes from

Attributed toWilliam J. O'Neil
SourceHow to Make Money in Stocks (CAN SLIM) (1988)

CAN SLIM made 'buy new highs, cut losses at 8%' a mantra for growth investors.

Rules, weights and thresholds are given as commonly cited. We have not re-checked them against the primary text.

The original rules

Buy leading stocks as they break out to new highs from a base, and sell if the price falls 7-8% below the purchase price.

How we replicate it

What we changed

Stock selection (earnings growth, market leaders, volume) cannot be applied to ETFs. Only the new-high entry and the 8% loss rule survive: buy when the close reaches a new 252-day closing high, sell if it closes 8% below the entry close, fixed 1/10 slot per market.

The exact rule we run

For each of the 10 markets, at the close: when flat and the close is at or above the highest close of the prior 252 days, go long; exit to cash when the close is at or below 92% of the entry close. Re-entry needs another new 252-day high. Fixed 1/10 slot per market.

Conventions: Position decided at a close earns the next day's return; no look-ahead; before costs; cash earns 0%; starts 2010-12-21, the first day a signal exists; every market has an equal slot of equity; indicators use each market's own trading days.

Growth of 1 vs the benchmark

Benchmark: Equal-weight of 10 markets, same period. Equal slots in S&P 500, Gold, Silver, Oil, Natural gas, Commodities, Agriculture, EUR/USD, Bitcoin, Ether, rebalanced daily; a market without data yet stays in cash.
