Antonacci Dual Momentum (SPY vs cash only): does it still work? Replicated through 2026

We re-ran it on S&P 500 from 2011-01-31 to 2026-11-10 (15.8 years, before costs): +10.7% a year, Sharpe 0.72, worst drawdown -33.7%. The benchmark, S&P 500 buy-and-hold, did +13.8% a year (Sharpe 0.84, worst drawdown -33.7%).

Verdict: Worse risk-adjusted return than its benchmark over this period. Historical, hypothetical results before costs, taxes and fees. Not investment advice.

Adapted Adapted: this is not the real thing

This is our adapted version, not the real portfolio or fund. Read what is lost before comparing it with anything published about the original.

What is lost

  • No international stocks, so the relative-momentum comparison does not exist.
  • When the signal is off the original rotates into aggregate bonds; here it sits in cash earning 0%.
Bond sleeve held as cash

When the signal is off, the original moves into bonds. We have no bond data, so this version sits in cash earning 0%.

What this means: no coupon income, no price gains when yields fall or stocks crash, and none of the losses bonds had when yields rose (2022). Read the results as the non-bond part plus idle cash. The real portfolio would have done differently, better in some years and worse in others.

Where it comes from

Attributed toGary Antonacci
SourceDual Momentum Investing (2014)

A rigorous, evidence-based rotation model that made 'dual momentum' a household phrase among systematic investors.

Rules, weights and thresholds are given as commonly cited. We have not re-checked them against the primary text.

The original rules

Monthly: compare US stocks and international stocks over 12 months (relative momentum); if the winner beats T-bills over 12 months (absolute momentum) hold it, otherwise hold aggregate bonds.

How we replicate it

What we changed

Only the absolute-momentum leg survives: hold SPY at month end if its 12-month return is positive (the T-bill hurdle is taken as 0), otherwise hold cash. The relative leg (US vs international stocks) is lost entirely, and the original's bond fallback is cash.

The exact rule we run

On the last trading day of each month: if the SPY month-end close is above its month-end close 12 months earlier, hold 100% SPY for the next month, otherwise 100% cash.

Conventions: Position decided at a close earns the next day's return; no look-ahead; before costs; cash earns 0%; starts 2011-01-31, the first day a signal exists; every market has an equal slot of equity; indicators use each market's own trading days.

How much to trust it

  • The rule changed its position only 12 times: with so few signals the results are anecdotal.
  • This is one backtest on one path of history, before costs and with hindsight on the choice of strategy. It does not show the strategy will work in future.

Honest caveat

Only the absolute-momentum half is replicated, so this is a plain SPY trend filter, not Antonacci's full model.
