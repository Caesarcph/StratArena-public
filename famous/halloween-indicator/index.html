Halloween Indicator (Sell in May): does it still work? Replicated through 2026

We re-ran it on S&P 500 from 2010-01-04 to 2026-11-10 (16.8 years, before costs): +7.7% a year, Sharpe 0.65, worst drawdown -33.7%. The benchmark, S&P 500 buy-and-hold, did +13.8% a year (Sharpe 0.85, worst drawdown -33.7%).

Verdict: Worse risk-adjusted return than its benchmark over this period. Historical, hypothetical results before costs, taxes and fees. Not investment advice.

Rule as published Rule as published

This replica applies the published rule as written, on the instrument we have. It is still a backtest on ETF data, before costs.

What is lost

  • One market (SPY) instead of the 37 countries studied.
  • Out-of-season money is cash earning 0%, not T-bills.

Where it comes from

Attributed toSven Bouman & Ben Jacobsen
SourceThe Halloween Indicator, 'Sell in May and Go Away': Another Puzzle, American Economic Review (2002)

The origin of the 'Sell in May and go away' market proverb, with academic backing.

Rules, weights and thresholds are given as commonly cited. We have not re-checked them against the primary text.

The original rules

Stocks earn most of their returns from November to April; hold equities in the winter half-year and stay out (cash) May to October.

How we replicate it

What we changed

Applied to SPY only; the paper studied 37 countries, and out-of-season money is held as cash at 0%.

The exact rule we run

Hold 100% SPY from the close of the last trading day of October to the close of the last trading day of April; hold cash from there until the last trading day of October.

Conventions: Position decided at a close earns the next day's return; no look-ahead; before costs; cash earns 0%; starts 2010-01-04, the first day a signal exists; every market has an equal slot of equity; indicators use each market's own trading days.

How much to trust it

  • 34 position changes over 16.8 years.
  • This is one backtest on one path of history, before costs and with hindsight on the choice of strategy. It does not show the strategy will work in future.

Honest caveat

Faithful for the rule, but only one US index instead of 37 markets.
