Connors RSI(2) Pullback: does it still work? Replicated through 2026

We re-ran it on S&P 500 from 2010-10-18 to 2026-11-12 (16.1 years, before costs): +4.3% a year, Sharpe 0.68, worst drawdown -14.7%. The benchmark, S&P 500 buy-and-hold, did +14.2% a year (Sharpe 0.87, worst drawdown -33.7%).

Verdict: Worse risk-adjusted return than its benchmark over this period. Historical, hypothetical results before costs, taxes and fees. Not investment advice.

Rule as published Rule as published

This replica applies the published rule as written, on the instrument we have. It is still a backtest on ETF data, before costs.

What is lost

  • Only SPY: the book tests several ETFs and stocks.
  • Entry thresholds differ between editions (RSI below 5 or below 10); we use 10.

Where it comes from

Attributed toLarry Connors & Cesar Alvarez
SourceShort Term Trading Strategies That Work (2008)

The best-known short-term mean-reversion recipe in retail trading, repeatedly cited in books and tested widely.

Rules, weights and thresholds are given as commonly cited. We have not re-checked them against the primary text.

The original rules

Buy an index ETF at the close when it is above its 200-day moving average and the 2-period RSI is below 10 (some versions 5); exit at the close when it closes above its 5-day moving average.

How we replicate it

What we changed

Applied to SPY only, long-only, as in the book's main index tests. Uses the standard Wilder RSI; thresholds follow the 10/5 version.

The exact rule we run

Signal on the SPY daily close. Enter 100% SPY when the close is above its 200-day average and Wilder RSI(2) is below 10. Exit to cash when the close is above its 5-day average. Otherwise hold the current state.

Conventions: Position decided at a close earns the next day's return; no look-ahead; before costs; cash earns 0%; starts 2010-10-18, the first day a signal exists; every market has an equal slot of equity; indicators use each market's own trading days.
